# player-psychology

## idle game engagement
- [behavior] After a big milestone, players who just reached it tend to keep playing in a fast section, so simulated reset timing becomes inaccurate. When: Just after a major milestone is reached. Why: Momentum keeps players in the run.
- [behavior] Random-timed optional bonuses that grant large temporary boosts encourage players to keep part of their attention on the game while idle players lose only the bonus. When: Designing optional active boosts with random spawn timing. Why: Unpredictable timing creates a hook for attention.
- [behavior] Players of idle games value anticipation and the return moment, since most of the game's activity happens while they are away. When: when the player is not expected to stay present. Why: Looking forward to a reward is itself a source of pleasure, so the return is the high point.
- [behavior] Clicker fatigue appears when input becomes tedious mashing, or when nothing remains but waiting, and players then tend to quit. When: Active input turns into repetitive mashing or only waiting remains. Why: Repetitive input and empty waiting drain player motivation.
- [heuristic] Seasonal events and double-output windows cost little to host and hand inactive players a dated reason to reopen the game. When: Players have lapsed and need a reason to return. Why: A dated window creates urgency and a concrete reason to come back.
- [pitfall] Penalizing time away through rotting stockpiles, lost streaks, or progress that accrues only while foregrounded breeds anxiety and eventually churn. When: Designing offline and return mechanics. Why: Players feel pressured and eventually quit.
