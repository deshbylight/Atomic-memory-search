# algorithms

## date deduction reasoning
- [rule] Confirm that a routine assumed to be fixed really holds before using it to exclude candidate dates. When: a recurring schedule such as weekly visits is used as a constraint. Why: an unverified routine can wrongly exclude the true date.
- [procedure] 1) Identify the facts the witness is certain of. 2) Use dated outside records to find the earliest and latest possible dates. 3) Remove candidate days that fail each routine or absence constraint. 4) Settle the remaining candidate with a fixed later event. When: testimony about dates is vague but independent records, schedules or calendars exist. Why: each elimination narrows the window until one candidate remains.
- [pitfall] Each elimination step depends on the one before it, so a single false premise invalidates every conclusion that follows from it. When: reasoning through a chain of date constraints. Why: links in a deduction chain are cumulative.
- [pitfall] Assuming an intrusion happened during the event window can mislead reasoning when the enabling action, such as removing a key, occurred much earlier. When: Reconstructing how a sealed space was entered. Why: The decisive action may predate the event by a long time.

## combining clue evidence
- [mechanism] A single common trait narrows the suspect pool little, but each independent, rarer trait multiplies the evidence, since the joint share of people matching all traits is tiny. When: many suspects match any one feature.
- [heuristic] Count only traits that are genuinely independent; two features from the same cause add little combined evidence.
- [pitfall] A vaguely recorded feature cannot be combined with others; clues must be noted precisely to be useful.

## height from shadow
- [heuristic] On a uniform surface such as open water, rely on a fixed landmark, since visual judgment of position is unreliable. Why: Water looks alike across its surface, so position cannot be judged by eye.
- [metric] An upright object's height equals a reference rod's height times the object's shadow length divided by the rod's shadow length, measured under the same sun angle. When: when both shadows are cast under the same light conditions. Why: Similar triangles make the two shadow ratios equal.
- [pitfall] Miscounting scale markers, or counting from a different start each time, shifts the intersection and the located position.

## locked room reasoning
- [procedure] 1) List every route into the sealed space and who held its key or watched it. 2) Find the route with no accounted-for control. 3) Reason backward: the key was removed before the event, not during it. 4) Fix the timeline with dated testimony. When: A sealed space seems impossible to enter and the record names keys, guards and times. Why: An unaccounted route usually reveals when the key was lost, which narrows the suspect list.
