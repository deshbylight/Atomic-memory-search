## K09-15

**A promised encounter creates an expectation to address.** A sequence can make an encounter feel necessary by establishing unresolved aims or consequences that lead toward it.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Scene, sequence, act and episode; Setup, payoff, foreshadowing and reveal; turning points.
- Apply: Name the meeting the preceding scenes prepare and the unresolved issue it must address.
- Limit: Addressing an expectation need not mean granting a wish; an omission needs a legible consequence.
- Check: Remove the encounter and inspect which established expectation is answered elsewhere or left unsupported.
- Requires [K09-11](Narrative.md#k09-11): An anticipated encounter needs enough prior information to form an expectation.
- Supports [K09-02](Narrative.md#k09-02): Opposed aims give the expected meeting an action beyond exchanging lore.

## K09-16

**A changed intention needs a credible process.** A consequential reversal of intention can need a visible process of persuasion, discovery or pressure rather than an unexplained jump between opposite attitudes.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: change; Motivation and credible action; Character arcs and stable characters.
- Apply: Identify what changes a character's decision and show the intermediate response.
- Limit: A short-term decision is not necessarily a complete personality conversion.
- Check: Compare the available evidence and motives before and after the scene; locate the step that makes the new choice credible.
- Constrains [K09-07](Narrative.md#k09-07): Learning a fact alone does not explain why the character adopts a new intention.
- Supports [K09-13](Narrative.md#k09-13): A changed decision should become visible in consequential conduct.

## K09-17

**Internal conflict can oppose motives within one person.** Opposition in a story can involve other people, environmental conditions or incompatible motives within one person; a named enemy is not the only source of conflict.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: conflicts; Desire, need, fear and values; Faction and personal conflicts.
- Apply: State the opposed aims or forces and the action through which their incompatibility becomes concrete.
- Limit: These categories can overlap and are not an exhaustive taxonomy or a requirement for combat.
- Check: Remove the antagonist and identify whether the unresolved incompatibility still produces a difficult choice.
- Requires [K09-02](Narrative.md#k09-02): Opposition needs identifiable wants and possible actions rather than disagreement as a label.
- Requires [K08-21](BeliefPractices.md#k08-21): When a prohibition creates the conflict, identify the obligation and the response to its breach.

## K09-18

**Earlier tone prepares the kind of climax.** Earlier events and descriptions can prepare the emotional character of a later crisis without disclosing its exact outcome.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: tone; Genre promises and tone.
- Apply: Place an early cue that makes a tragic, comic or uncanny culmination belong to the same story.
- Limit: Contrast can be deliberate; tonal preparation does not require every scene to have the same mood.
- Check: Compare the opening cues with the climax and explain any apparent change of genre or emotional promise.
- Constrains [K09-11](Narrative.md#k09-11): Knowing the emotional register does not disclose the outcome or remove uncertainty.
- Supports [K09-15](DramaticScenes.md#k09-15): The emotional promise helps frame what an anticipated encounter can satisfy.
