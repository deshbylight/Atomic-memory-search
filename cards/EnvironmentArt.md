## K12-01

**Art massing preserves spatial decisions.** Large grouped masses and intervals establish hierarchy before small detail; the same detail distribution reads differently as viewpoint changes.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: terrain masses; large divisions.
- Apply: Align the dominant environmental masses with the intended route and activity, then add architectural detail in subordinate clusters.
- Limit: This does not mean every route must be the brightest object or every location needs one centered focal point.
- Check: Compare blockout and art views at matched camera positions; identify any lost route or encounter cue.
- Requires [K10-01](VisualLanguage.md#k10-01): Detail distribution must preserve the chosen primary and subordinate readings.

## K12-02

**Surface variation needs a cause and a scale.** Plant distribution responds to local conditions, while material roughness controls reflected appearance at a different scale from large surface shape.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: vegetation; material identity.
- Apply: Separate geological form, growth patches, use wear and material response instead of applying one noise pattern to all of them.
- Limit: A plausible material hypothesis remains visually unverified until seen under representative lighting and distance.
- Check: Explain why one area is bare, worn or reflective and why its neighbour differs.
- Requires [K07-03](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-03): Local light, water and disturbance explain natural patch boundaries.
- Requires [K12-04](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-04): Surface variation is acceptable only if integrated dressing preserves the necessary route and action cues.

## K12-03

**Prop families record production and occupation.** Repeated forms with variation can communicate shared construction; later use and repair can change members of the same family.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: construction; activity traces.
- Apply: Give props a common making method and distinguish role, owner and age through localized changes; place them along plausible activity routes.
- Limit: A repeated motif alone does not prove culture, and random debris does not explain an event sequence.
- Check: Name the activity that put each major cluster there and distinguish original, worn and repaired features.
- Requires [K08-03](Societies.md#k08-03): Material culture supplies reasons for shared construction and later adaptation.
- Supports [K09-04](Narrative.md#k09-04): Localized original, worn and repaired props can support inference about a sequence of occupation.
- Supports [K12-15](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-15): A prop family can share layered construction even when the exposed facing differs.

## K12-04

**Acceptance happens after integration.** Composition and contrast depend on surrounding elements; adding detail or effects can change which object attracts attention.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: gameplay space; context review.
- Apply: Review environment art with gameplay cues, characters and effects present at representative player distances.
- Limit: An attractive isolated asset is not evidence that the encounter remains readable or that its runtime cost fits.
- Check: Compare key cue detection before and after dressing, including a low-contrast and an occluded view.
- Requires [K15-03](InteractionAndAccess.md#k15-03): Essential information needs more than a single fragile visual distinction.

## K12-05

**Talus should have an upstream source.** Rock fragments released from an exposed slope can accumulate below it; source material constrains their character.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Rock type, strata, joints and fracture; Cliffs, talus, caves and overhangs; Erosion, deposition and soil cover.
- Apply: Relate debris material and concentration to the face that supplied it.
- Limit: Transport and later disturbance can move fragments away from the original fall.
- Check: Identify a source, route and accumulation zone for each major debris mass.
- Requires [K07-05](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-05): Freeze-thaw is one possible release mechanism and needs its own conditions.

## K12-06

**Weathering should distinguish residual material.** Materials with different mineral composition can survive the same exposure differently.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Stone, brick, plaster and earth; Oxidation, corrosion and chemical decay; Cracking, fracture, collapse and debris; Rock type, strata, joints and fracture.
- Apply: Use selective recession or surviving bands only when the material explanation supports them.
- Limit: Color variation alone does not prove a chemical difference.
- Check: Explain what was removed and what remained under the proposed exposure.
- Requires [K07-07](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-07): Chemical alteration depends on mineral composition.
- Supports [K11-04](BuiltWorld.md#k11-04): Selective loss contributes evidence about the phases visible in a surviving ruin.

## K12-07

**A drawer joint answers a pulling force.** Opening a drawer pulls its front away from its sides; interlocking joints can resist that separation.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: construction; Object purpose and user action; Assembly, joints, fasteners and load paths; Wood species, grain, cutting and finishing.
- Apply: Relate visible joinery to handling, assembly and likely failure.
- Limit: A decorative imitation of a joint does not establish structural behavior.
- Check: Trace the pulling force from the handle through the front to the side pieces.
- Supports [K12-03](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-03): Prop families should record production and use rather than arbitrary seams.
- Supports [K12-13](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-13): Withdrawal resistance can be explained by a visible retaining contact.

## K12-08

**Wood movement needs allowance at attachments.** Wood parts can shrink and warp; attachment and grain direction influence whether a panel can move without opening a damaging gap.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Storage, maintenance, repair and reuse; Wood species, grain, cutting and finishing; Transitions, seams and layering; Variation from exposure and maintenance.
- Apply: Show how a lid, bottom or panel is retained while allowing movement.
- Limit: The amount depends on species, moisture and construction; do not assign one universal gap.
- Check: Check grain direction and identify the joint that would bind or separate first.
- Constrains [K12-07](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-07): A working joint must tolerate the material's dimensional behavior.

## K12-09

**A lid type changes handling and visible construction.** A sliding lid uses grooves and a receiving path, while a removable or hinged lid has different retention needs.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Tools, containers, furniture and machinery; Ergonomics, handling and carrying; object relationships.
- Apply: Choose lid operation before placing hinges, seams and storage clearances.
- Limit: An attractive closed prop does not prove usable opening or carrying.
- Check: Trace removal, access and replacement without intersecting surrounding objects.
- Supports [K11-07](BuiltWorld.md#k11-07): Opening mechanisms need space for their complete operation.

## K12-10

**Textile appearance can come from weave or applied ornament.** Weave structure and relief can produce a visible pattern; applied ornament provides another source of pattern.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Cloth, leather, paper and woven structures; Texture scale and lighting dependence; Architectural ornament tied to craft.
- Apply: Distinguish seams, yarn direction, raised weave and applied motif in material descriptions.
- Limit: Extra texture frequency can obscure the larger form at play distance.
- Check: Reduce the view until fine weave disappears and check whether the material family still reads.
- Requires [K10-10](VisualLanguage.md#k10-10): Relief can change apparent color without changing the dye.

## K12-11

**Overbank deposits should follow the flood's changing flow.** Water spreading across a floodplain generally slows; deposited material can be coarser near the channel and finer farther away.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: damp and deposits; Rain, splash, runoff and moisture paths; Dust, soot, sediment and staining; Routine and exceptional events.
- Apply: Differentiate bank deposits from more distant fine sediment in a flood aftermath.
- Limit: Local obstructions, repeated floods and later disturbance can complicate this pattern.
- Check: Trace the overflow route and explain changes in sediment texture instead of applying a uniform mud layer.
- Requires [K07-12](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-12): The deposit depends on selective entrainment and settling.
- Supports [K12-02](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-02): Localized exposure gives surface variation a physical cause.

## K12-12

**Intersections can order damage and repair.** A cut through a patch implies a different sequence from a patch covering the cut.

- Kind: design inference. Focus: Construction phases and later alterations; Transitions, seams and layering; Event order in overlapping damage; Cause, consequence and chronology.
- Apply: Place seams, repairs and fractures so their intersections preserve an intended event order.
- Limit: Design inference: missing or reused material can leave several histories possible; surface age is not an exact clock.
- Check: Reverse two events in a sketch and identify which visible contact would have to change.
- Requires [K07-14](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-14): Relative chronology separates ordering evidence from an absolute date.
- Supports [K11-04](BuiltWorld.md#k11-04): Local contacts can distinguish construction and reuse phases.
- Supports [K09-04](Narrative.md#k09-04): Consistent overlap supplies evidence for a recoverable narrative sequence.
- Supports [K09-10](Narrative.md#k09-10): Observed event order can conflict with a witness account.

## K12-13

**Joint retention and disassembly depend on the key.** A mortise receives a tenon; pins or wedges can resist withdrawal. A projecting tenon with a removable key can permit dismantling that a permanently fixed joint does not.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: construction; Tools, containers, furniture and machinery; Assembly, joints, fasteners and load paths; Storage, maintenance, repair and reuse.
- Apply: Explain visible pegs, wedges and protrusions through retention and an assembly or repair sequence.
- Limit: Not every wedge arrangement is removable; hidden fasteners and glue can change the sequence.
- Check: Trace the direction of separation, the retaining contact and the access needed to release a supposedly removable part.
- Supports [K12-07](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-07): Retaining geometry can be judged against the force that separates members.
- Requires [K12-08](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-08): Wood movement must remain compatible with the chosen attachment.

## K12-14

**A board's surface grain depends on how it was cut.** Tangential and radial cuts expose annual growth rings differently; the visible grain of a board is related to its orientation within the log.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Wood species, grain, cutting and finishing; construction.
- Apply: Draw the cut through a log before assigning grain to a board's face, edge and exposed end.
- Limit: Species and processing affect appearance; historical cutting labels vary, and surface grain alone does not establish exact species, age or strength.
- Check: Compare face and end views and reject a grain pattern that cannot follow the proposed growth-ring orientation.
- Supports [K12-08](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-08): Cut orientation helps explain dimensional behavior that an attachment must accommodate.
- Supports [K12-03](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-03): Consistent cutting choices can distinguish related manufactured objects from random material variation.

## K12-15

**A visible wood facing need not be the structural core.** A constructed door can have a wooden core covered by a separate veneer; its visible outer wood need not describe its full thickness.

- Kind: design inference. Focus: Assembly, joints, fasteners and load paths; material identity; Orthographic views and construction callouts.
- Apply: Use a section or cutaway to distinguish facing, core and joints before depicting a chipped edge or repair.
- Limit: Design inference: layered construction is one possibility, not a rule for every wooden object; a thin facing alone does not establish low quality or strength.
- Check: Trace a damaged edge through the proposed layers and check whether the exposed core agrees with the section.
- Supports [K12-03](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-03): Layered construction offers a making method that can recur across a prop family.
- Constrains [K12-12](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-12): Damage and repair evidence must respect which layer existed when each cut or patch was made.
