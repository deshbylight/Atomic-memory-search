## K11-01

**Programme explains form.** Built space accommodates activities and circulation; an architectural type communicates more when access and use agree with its visible structure.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: building purpose; spatial sequence.
- Apply: List occupants, work, storage and service movements before selecting arches, towers or a room outline.
- Limit: Historical types are options, not mandatory solutions; gameplay access and the approved setting can require explicit departures.
- Check: Trace an occupant, a bulky supply and waste through the proposed space.
- Supports [K06-03](SpatialDesign.md#k06-03): Functional circulation can become meaningful encounter access and obstruction.

## K11-02

**Settlement networks expose priorities.** A settlement connects dwellings, work and services; changing access or concentrating exchange changes how inhabitants use those connections.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: streets and paths; social meaning.
- Apply: Place essential services and their supply routes, then distinguish public, restricted and maintenance access.
- Limit: An ideal historical town plan is not a universal model; terrain, power and repeated player use require their own checks.
- Check: Trace a daily service loop for inhabitants with different access privileges.
- Requires [K08-04](Societies.md#k08-04): Different ordinary users prevent a settlement from becoming one decorative cultural stereotype.
- Supports [K11-14](BuiltWorld.md#k11-14): The routes supplying essential services can make allocation priorities visible.

## K11-03

**Underground form needs support and water logic.** Loads need a credible route into support; unsupported upper masses and changing ground or water conditions can undermine that route.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: load paths; drainage.
- Apply: Draw a rough section showing support, openings, drainage and maintained access before ornamenting a subterranean structure.
- Limit: This is plausibility guidance, not structural safety advice or a runtime destruction model.
- Check: Point to what carries each large overhead mass and where water can collect or leave.
- Requires [K07-01](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-01): Water paths constrain the plausibility of underground access and maintenance.
- Constrains [K12-02](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-02): If water enters the occupied structure, visible surface patches should agree with its storage and exit paths.
- Supports [K11-11](BuiltWorld.md#k11-11): Following a load path includes checking the bearing below upper masonry supports.
- Requires [K11-13](BuiltWorld.md#k11-13): When a wall retains ground, explain lateral loading as well as the vertical support route.

## K11-04

**Ruins contain phases.** Construction, use, repair, abandonment and reuse leave different spatial relationships; collapse should follow what lost support.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: reoccupation; failure sequences.
- Apply: Distinguish original fabric, damage and later occupation; arrange debris and new passages according to those phases.
- Limit: Do not assume all old structures are abandoned or every broken surface is reachable or destructible in play.
- Check: Explain a blocked opening, a repaired threshold and a debris pile through one consistent sequence.
- Supports [K12-03](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-03): Dressing can communicate the sequence through localized traces rather than uniform damage.
- Supports [K12-12](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-12): Repair and damage intersections can distinguish occupation phases.

## K11-05

**An arch needs a path for lateral thrust.** A masonry arch transfers compression into its supports and can impose lateral thrust that must be resisted.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: load paths; Load paths and stability; Walls, columns, piers and buttresses; Arches, vaults, domes and roofs; Spans, openings and support thickness.
- Apply: Explain the visible supports before choosing a large masonry opening.
- Limit: This conceptual account does not establish dimensions or structural safety.
- Check: Trace the proposed support path to the ground, including what resists outward movement.
- Requires [K11-03](BuiltWorld.md#k11-03): Support must agree with ground and water conditions.
- Supports [K11-12](BuiltWorld.md#k11-12): Compression through an arch can redirect loading around an opening.

## K11-06

**Vault form changes the support and opening pattern.** A barrel vault and intersecting vaults distribute support demands differently, changing where openings can be placed.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: openings and spans; Orders, bays, modules and repetition; Light, shadow and opening design; Arches, vaults, domes and roofs.
- Apply: Relate bay rhythm, piers and openings to the selected construction family.
- Limit: A decorative rib pattern alone does not prove a working load path.
- Check: Compare the plan, section and exterior support rhythm for contradictions.
- Requires [K11-05](BuiltWorld.md#k11-05): Vault supports must resolve the same thrust question as their arches.

## K11-07

**Door operation occupies usable space.** A hinged leaf sweeps floor space; a sliding leaf needs an adjacent receiving zone or pocket.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: circulation; Entrances, thresholds and transitional spaces; Courts, halls, corridors, stairs and ramps; Architectural affordances and gameplay readability.
- Apply: Show door operation together with furniture, approach and circulation.
- Limit: A visually clear opening can become obstructed when the door is operated.
- Check: Trace approach, opening, passage and closing with the room in use.
- Requires [K06-07](SpatialDesign.md#k06-07): Functional clearance depends on the actual action and dimensions.

## K11-08

**Decoration competes with furniture for attention.** Dense objects and elaborate backgrounds can compete; the character of the room depends on their relation.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Facade and interior hierarchy; Material, texture and ornament; Room purpose and functional adjacency.
- Apply: Review wall divisions and openings together with the intended furnishings.
- Limit: Historical taste prescriptions are not universal rules of beauty.
- Check: Compare a furnished view with its empty architectural view at the target camera.
- Requires [K10-01](VisualLanguage.md#k10-01): Visual hierarchy is relational rather than an isolated asset property.

## K11-09

**A window needs a usable path to the sky.** Neighboring walls, upper floors, lintels and beams can obstruct the sky seen through an opening.

- Kind: design inference. Focus: openings and spans; Light, shadow and opening design; Light and acoustic cues.
- Apply: Check a section from the intended receiving area through the opening before treating a window as a daylight source.
- Limit: Design inference: sky visibility establishes access, not a sufficient illuminance or a validated lighting result.
- Check: Trace the obstruction line, then compare the proposed receiving area under the intended sky and surface conditions.
- Requires [K11-01](BuiltWorld.md#k11-01): Daylight provision should serve the room's activity.
- Requires [K10-02](VisualLanguage.md#k10-02): Visible light distribution also depends on material response.

## K11-10

**Service circulation has loaded users.** Routes used by people carrying burdens create passing and visibility demands that empty-handed traversal can miss.

- Kind: design inference. Focus: Building users, activities and daily cycles; Horizontal and vertical circulation; Accessibility, service access and evacuation; Storage, workshops, sanitation and maintenance.
- Apply: Trace a delivery from entrance to storage through turns, stairs and occupied passages.
- Limit: Design inference: this requires a stated load and traffic scenario, not a universal corridor width.
- Check: Test opposing loaded users at the least visible junction; identify where they can see and pass each other.
- Requires [K11-07](BuiltWorld.md#k11-07): The load must also fit through the operating doorway.
- Supports [K06-07](SpatialDesign.md#k06-07): Clearance needs to be evaluated for the activity actually performed.

## K11-11

**Upper masonry supports need a credible bearing below.** In load-bearing masonry, upper walls, piers and columns need a load path into substructures and ground; an upper support cannot be justified solely by its visible outline.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: foundations and ground; Load paths and stability; Foundations and ground conditions; support.
- Apply: Compare support positions between storeys and include the ground-bearing part when proposing buried rooms or an exposed foundation.
- Limit: Alignment is one plausibility check; it does not determine foundation dimensions or prove structural stability.
- Check: Trace an upper support down through a section and identify any unsupported gap or unexplained transfer across a span.
- Requires [K11-03](BuiltWorld.md#k11-03): The proposed support route must agree with ground and water conditions.
- Constrains [K06-11](SpatialDesign.md#k06-11): Vertical arrangements must leave space for the supports they imply.

## K11-12

**A relieving arch can redirect load around a lintel.** A masonry arch above an opening can route load toward its side supports instead of leaving all of it on the lintel below.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Walls, columns, piers and buttresses; Arches, vaults, domes and roofs; Spans, openings and support thickness; repair.
- Apply: Distinguish a load-relieving construction from an arch-shaped decoration and show how its supports relate to the opening.
- Limit: An arch still needs working bearings and resistance to thrust; its outline alone does not establish which loads bypass the lintel.
- Check: Trace the proposed load route through the arch and its bearings; explain the lintel's remaining role and any intended replacement sequence.
- Requires [K11-05](BuiltWorld.md#k11-05): The relieving arch still needs support for compression and lateral thrust.
- Supports [K12-13](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-13): An intended replacement sequence also depends on how the removable member is retained.

## K11-13

**Retained ground can load a wall sideways.** An earth-retaining substructure must resist pressure from the material behind it as well as loads from above; wet conditions can change that loading.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: load paths; Foundations and ground conditions; support; drainage.
- Apply: Distinguish a freestanding partition from a wall holding back ground when explaining its thickness, reinforcement and relationship to water.
- Limit: A visible buttress does not prove stability, and appearance alone does not establish the properties or saturation of the retained material.
- Check: Draw both sides of the wall and trace retained ground, water paths and the supports resisting sideways loading.
- Requires [K07-08](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-08): Wetness and saturation can change the behavior of the retained material.
- Constrains [K11-03](BuiltWorld.md#k11-03): A vertical load path alone cannot justify a wall that also retains ground.

## K11-14

**Separate supply branches can expose allocation priorities.** A water-distribution layout can separate branches serving public collection points, communal facilities and private consumers, making their access relationships explicit.

- Kind: design inference. Focus: infrastructure; water and sanitation.
- Apply: For a settlement concept, trace each user from a shared source and identify which withdrawals could reduce another user's supply.
- Limit: Design inference: separate branches do not alone guarantee priority or adequate flow; capacity, water level and connections still matter.
- Check: Trace a low-supply case and a disputed private withdrawal; show whether the supposed public allocation can actually be bypassed.
- Requires [K11-02](BuiltWorld.md#k11-02): Essential services need explicit users and supply routes.
- Supports [K08-13](Societies.md#k08-13): Physical distribution can reveal who controls collection and allocation.
