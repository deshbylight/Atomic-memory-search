## K08-20

**Sacred status changes how something may be approached.** A community can set a place, object or activity apart through prescribed handling and access; sacred status is a relationship expressed in practice.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: religion; Sacred places, objects and pilgrimage.
- Apply: Specify who may approach a shrine, what preparation is expected and how ordinary handling differs.
- Limit: Precious material, age or visual grandeur alone does not establish sacred status.
- Check: Compare the same action inside and outside the sacred context; identify who recognizes a difference.
- Requires [K08-06](Societies.md#k08-06): A sacred distinction needs observable practices rather than an ornament alone.
- Requires [K08-05](Societies.md#k08-05): Participant meanings establish why a handling rule matters.

## K08-21

**A taboo can prescribe prevention and repair.** A taboo can prohibit an act while related rituals prevent a breach or address its perceived consequences; the prohibition and its repair need separate description.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: taboo; Taboos, purity and obligations.
- Apply: Name the forbidden contact, expected danger and any accepted response after a breach.
- Limit: A feared consequence is a participant belief, not proof of a physical hazard or a universal punishment.
- Check: Trace an accidental breach and identify who recognizes the problem and what could restore ordinary relations.
- Requires [K08-20](BeliefPractices.md#k08-20): The prohibited handling must be related to what participants set apart.
- Supports [K08-17](CulturalChange.md#k08-17): A response to a breach can combine ritual repair with informal or official sanctions.

## K08-22

**A cosmology explains an order as well as an origin.** An origin account can locate humans, other beings and powers within a meaningful order, connecting a creation story with present identity or obligations.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Cosmology and sacred narratives; Myth, folklore and superstition.
- Apply: Connect one account of beginnings to a current duty or relationship.
- Limit: A community account does not establish the fictional universe's objective history or uniform agreement.
- Check: Remove the origin account and identify which present explanation or obligation loses its stated basis.
- Requires [K08-05](Societies.md#k08-05): The account must be interpreted through participants' learned meanings.
- Supports [K08-06](Societies.md#k08-06): An account of cosmic order can explain a practiced obligation.

## K08-23

**A funeral can address the deceased and the living.** Funeral practices can combine beliefs about a deceased person's passage with comfort and communal support for survivors.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: ritual; Death beliefs, burial and memorialization.
- Apply: Distinguish the intended benefit to the deceased from the work of supporting mourners.
- Limit: Burial objects alone do not prove one precise afterlife doctrine; mourners may interpret a ceremony differently.
- Check: Describe who prepares, attends and provides support, then separate their actions from claims about an afterlife.
- Requires [K08-06](Societies.md#k08-06): Death beliefs must be connected to what participants actually do.
- Supports [K08-15](Societies.md#k08-15): Collective support can reinforce belonging alongside an individual transition.
