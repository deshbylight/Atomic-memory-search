## K02-01

**Useful cues are distinctive and retrievable.** Remembering depends on retrieval cues as well as storage; one cue associated with many competing meanings becomes less diagnostic.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Encoding, retrieval, forgetting and memory cues.
- Apply: Give important routes a distinctive combination of shape, position and function, then reuse that combination when recall matters.
- Limit: More decoration or more repeated icons can increase interference instead of improving orientation.
- Check: After a delay ask a player to identify the route without a highlighted objective.
- Supports [K06-02](SpatialDesign.md#k06-02): A landmark only helps navigation if it identifies a location or decision.
- Requires [K02-15](PlayerExperience.md#k02-15): When appearance and spacing identify a recall cue, check whether they imply the intended grouping.

## K02-02

**Choice framing changes perceived stakes.** Judgements can depend on gain or loss framing, salient recent examples and omitted alternatives rather than probability alone.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: biases and bounded rationality; loss aversion.
- Apply: Present the recoverable cost, possible gain and unknown information before a risky action; compare equivalent framings in a probe.
- Limit: These are tendencies, not a deterministic model of every player or permission to manipulate spending.
- Check: Can a player explain the consequence without relying on a spectacular but rare previous event?
- Constrains [K04-03](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-03): Odds and outcomes need interpretation as well as numeric balance.

## K02-03

**Learning includes retrieval and transfer.** Practising retrieval and connecting new information to existing knowledge can improve later access; recognizing an answer is different from producing it.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: practice and transfer.
- Apply: Teach an interaction safely, later remove the prompt, then ask for the same principle in a different setting.
- Limit: Successful prompted repetition alone does not demonstrate independent understanding.
- Check: Observe whether the player solves a new instance without repeating the tutorial wording.
- Supports [K15-02](InteractionAndAccess.md#k15-02): Onboarding should progress from demonstration to independent use.
- Supports [K02-10](PlayerExperience.md#k02-10): Spacing separates repeated opportunities for independent retrieval.
- Supports [K02-13](PlayerExperience.md#k02-13): Independent retrieval after a demonstration helps distinguish remembered content from recognition.

## K02-04

**Test participants and group context matter.** People differ in prior knowledge and capabilities; a group adds mutual influence and coordination demands absent from individual performance.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: expertise; group influence.
- Apply: Separate novice, experienced and cooperative observations instead of treating their average as one representative player.
- Limit: A small convenience sample cannot establish population prevalence or accessibility for everyone.
- Check: Report who participated, their prior experience and which difficulties differed between participants.
- Requires [K16-03](ResearchAndProduction.md#k16-03): Evaluation conclusions must remain within the tested sample and conditions.

## K02-05

**Multisensory cues need event agreement.** Signals from different senses can be integrated as one event; spatial and temporal conflicts can make their association ambiguous.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: multisensory integration; Visual, auditory, tactile and vestibular perception; Multimodal integration and conflicting sensory cues.
- Apply: Align the visible source, sound onset and tactile response for an event.
- Limit: Extra modalities do not guarantee extra clarity, especially when one signal already dominates.
- Check: Ask which event produced the cue with each channel alone and together.
- Supports [K14-04](Audiovisual.md#k14-04): Coordinated effects should describe the same event.

## K02-06

**Reinforcement and punishment describe different changes.** Reinforcement increases a response; punishment reduces it. Adding a stimulus and removing one are separate dimensions.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Classical and operant conditioning; reinforcement schedules; feedback.
- Apply: Describe the behavior being encouraged and the consequence that follows it.
- Limit: A reward's intention does not prove that players repeat the intended behavior.
- Check: Observe whether the target action becomes more frequent rather than counting rewards delivered.
- Constrains [K04-01](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-01): Reward presentation and learned behavior need separate observations.

## K02-07

**Shaping teaches intermediate actions.** Successive approximations can establish a complex behavior before the full sequence is required.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: demonstration; scaffolding; learning from failure; Practice, skill acquisition, feedback and error recovery.
- Apply: Give feedback for an attainable intermediate step, then combine it with the next step.
- Limit: Removing support too early can turn learning into repeated unexplained failure.
- Check: Check whether a novice can perform the next variation without repeating the demonstration.
- Supports [K02-03](PlayerExperience.md#k02-03): Practice must transfer beyond the original example.

## K02-08

**Recognition and execution are different evidence.** Recognizing an answer, recalling it without prompts and executing a learned procedure test different forms of knowledge.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: mental models; Mental models, expectations and transfer of learning; practice and transfer.
- Apply: Test both identifying an affordance and using it in a changed situation.
- Limit: Verbal explanation alone does not demonstrate procedural competence.
- Check: Ask a player to act after removing the tutorial prompt.
- Constrains [K15-02](InteractionAndAccess.md#k15-02): Information hierarchy should support the kind of retrieval the task requires.

## K02-09

**An audience changes novice and practiced performance.** The presence of others can help familiar performance while hindering a difficult or poorly learned task.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Novice and expert differences; expertise.
- Apply: Compare learning alone, with friends and under perceived evaluation.
- Limit: Group presence does not affect every task or participant equally.
- Check: Separate coordination errors from mistakes already present in the solo task.
- Supports [K02-04](PlayerExperience.md#k02-04): Participant experience and social context limit generalization.

## K02-10

**Separate practice across time.** Distributed practice can support later retention better than concentrating the same practice into one uninterrupted period.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Encoding, retrieval, forgetting and memory cues; Practice, skill acquisition, feedback and error recovery.
- Apply: Revisit a learned operation after intervening activity and test it without the original prompt.
- Limit: Spacing benefits depend on the task and retention interval; no universal interval follows.
- Check: Compare delayed independent performance after equal practice time with different spacing.
- Requires [K02-08](PlayerExperience.md#k02-08): The delayed check must distinguish recognition from independent execution.

## K02-11

**Similar learning can interfere in both directions.** Older learning can interfere with newer learning; newer learning can also impede retrieval of older material, especially when their associations compete.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: inference and misconception; Encoding, retrieval, forgetting and memory cues; Mental models, expectations and transfer of learning.
- Apply: Contrast similar rules and test both the newly taught operation and the earlier one afterward.
- Limit: A failed recall does not establish that the stored knowledge was erased.
- Check: After teaching the second rule, test which rule participants apply in each context.
- Requires [K02-01](PlayerExperience.md#k02-01): Distinctive cues help separate competing associations.
- Supports [K16-03](ResearchAndProduction.md#k16-03): Competing learned associations can produce carryover when condition order changes.
- Constrains [K02-14](PlayerExperience.md#k02-14): A missing cue response can reflect extinction rather than competition between memories.

## K02-12

**Visibility does not establish attention.** Concentrating on one task can leave an unexpected visible event unnoticed.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Salience, selective attention and visual search; Change blindness and inattentional blindness.
- Apply: Evaluate an essential cue during the task that competes for attention, rather than in an isolated screenshot.
- Limit: A conspicuous isolated cue can still fail during a demanding task; repeated exposure changes expectations.
- Check: Present the event during representative play and ask what was noticed before explaining the cue.
- Constrains [K14-04](Audiovisual.md#k14-04): Concurrent effects can compete with the event that needs attention.
- Requires [K15-09](InteractionAndAccess.md#k15-09): Attention must be available while the information can still guide a decision.

## K02-13

**Watching a demonstration does not establish learning.** Observational learning involves attending to a model, retaining the action, being able to reproduce it and having a reason to perform it.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Habituation, sensitization and observational learning; demonstration; practice and transfer.
- Apply: After a demonstrated action, distinguish failure to notice, remember, execute or choose the action instead of assuming that replaying the demonstration solves every failure.
- Limit: These stages are an explanatory model; one observed failure does not identify its cause or establish a universal teaching sequence.
- Check: Ask the learner to explain and perform the action without the model, then compare the explanation with the attempted execution.
- Requires [K02-12](PlayerExperience.md#k02-12): The relevant action must receive attention, not merely appear on screen.
- Requires [K02-08](PlayerExperience.md#k02-08): Recognizing the demonstration differs from independently executing its procedure.

## K02-14

**An extinguished cue response can return after a pause.** A conditioned response can weaken when its cue repeatedly occurs without the paired event; after a rest period, the response can reappear.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Classical and operant conditioning; reinforcement schedules; Mental models, expectations and transfer of learning.
- Apply: When revising an established cue-event pairing, compare immediate responses with responses after an interruption.
- Limit: This concerns conditioned responses, not a guarantee about deliberate strategy, complete forgetting or every repeated cue.
- Check: Present the changed pairing, then revisit the cue after a pause; distinguish reduced immediate response from durable disappearance.
- Constrains [K02-11](PlayerExperience.md#k02-11): A missing response does not by itself establish interference between competing memories.
- Requires [K16-03](ResearchAndProduction.md#k16-03): Practice order and the interval before retesting can change the observed result.

## K02-15

**Proximity and similarity can imply a group.** Nearby elements and elements with similar appearance tend to be perceived together; a perceived group can differ from an intended functional category.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: perceptual grouping; Perceptual grouping, depth and spatial orientation.
- Apply: Compare spacing and appearance when players mistake nearby unrelated objects for one group or separate related objects.
- Limit: Grouping is a perceptual tendency, not proof of shared function; context and competing cues can change the reading.
- Check: Ask observers to mark the groups they see before explaining the intended categories.
- Supports [K10-01](VisualLanguage.md#k10-01): Perceptual grouping helps explain how separate elements form a compositional mass.
- Constrains [K15-02](InteractionAndAccess.md#k15-02): An information hierarchy can imply the wrong categories through spacing or similarity.
