## K13-01

**A body must support its activity.** Locomotion uses support and swing phases; body balance and contact depend on the movement and the surfaces being crossed.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: balance; habitat.
- Apply: Design a creature around habitat, contact points and a resource-acquiring activity before choosing ornament or a combat label.
- Limit: A fantasy anatomy can depart from ordinary biomechanics, but its support or propulsion exception must be explained.
- Check: Sketch standing, turning and crossing a representative obstacle without changing body proportions between poses.
- Requires [K07-02](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-02): The proposed habitat and dependencies determine which activities the body must support.
- Constrains [K13-04](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-04): When a creature activity becomes an encounter, its body must support the visible action and commitment.

## K13-02

**Equipment changes silhouette and action.** Carried objects alter what a body can hold, display and move around; shared construction and repairs can communicate use and affiliation.

- Kind: design inference. Focus: equipment silhouette; social role.
- Apply: Give costume and equipment a role in work, carrying and recognition; show the loaded silhouette while performing the core action.
- Limit: Decorative affiliation is not proof of allegiance, and a concept does not establish sockets, collision or equipment implementation.
- Check: Can another player recognize the intended role while the character turns and performs an action?
- Supports [K08-03](Societies.md#k08-03): Construction and repair details connect character identity to material life.
- Requires [K13-07](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-07): A costume silhouette needs a plausible origin for its cloth volume.

## K13-03

**Contacts and timing carry motion.** Walk cycles depend on contact, weight transfer and coordination, not just the most widely separated leg poses; support and swing limbs serve different jobs.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: weight; timing.
- Apply: Block key contacts and body shifts before adding secondary motion or stylistic exaggeration.
- Limit: One gait template does not fit every creature, speed or surface; stylization still needs deliberate contacts.
- Check: Inspect planted feet, support changes and body displacement over a complete cycle.
- Requires [K03-01](ActionsAndCombat.md#k03-01): Animation timing must agree with the movement and acceleration it presents.
- Supports [K13-11](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-11): A movement trajectory can be compared separately from its contact animation.
- Supports [K13-15](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-15): A horse trot supplies a concrete diagonal-pair case for a contact timeline.

## K13-04

**Gameplay motion exposes a decision window.** An action has preparation, movement and a resulting state; its visible motion can reveal or obscure when another participant can respond.

- Kind: design inference. Focus: telegraphs; in-engine acceptance.
- Apply: For an action concept identify the cue, commitment and recovery, then compare the intended response with what the moving silhouette communicates.
- Limit: No timing, invulnerability, hit window or defensive mechanic becomes approved through an animation sketch.
- Check: Ask a new observer to identify the action and its commitment before showing the outcome.
- Supports [K14-04](Audiovisual.md#k14-04): Coordinated effects and sound can clarify the same event or obscure it through conflict.

## K13-05

**Foot posture changes the limb silhouette.** Plantigrade support includes the sole; digitigrade and unguligrade support raise different portions of the foot from the ground.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: skeletal structure; Skeleton, joints and surface landmarks; Body plan and limb specialization; Locomotion, balance and support.
- Apply: Mark anatomical joints and ground contacts before exaggerating leg proportions.
- Limit: These categories do not determine one universal gait or authorize an arbitrary hybrid joint.
- Check: Compare the standing skeleton with the silhouette and identify the ankle rather than guessing from the bend.
- Requires [K13-01](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-01): Ground contact must support the proposed activity.

## K13-06

**The shoulder participates in a quadruped stride.** In a quadruped's forelimb movement, scapular motion accompanies flexion and extension of the limb.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Joint range, compression and extension; Weight transfer, counterbalance and recovery; Creature locomotion/contact diagrams.
- Apply: Include shoulder mass movement when blocking the reach and support phases.
- Limit: Different species have different constraints; a human arm template is insufficient.
- Check: Track the scapula, upper limb and contact point through one stride.
- Supports [K13-03](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-03): Contact and body coordination should be established before polish.

## K13-07

**Garment fullness has a construction origin.** Pleats, gathers and cut pieces distribute cloth volume from seams or attachment points.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: construction; Silhouette and garment layers; Cut, drape, folds and textile weight; Fastenings, seams, belts and supports.
- Apply: Explain where fabric is collected, released and supported before adding folds.
- Limit: One historical garment family does not define all clothing or all fabric behavior.
- Check: Trace a major fold mass back to cut, seam, fastening or support.
- Requires [K12-10](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-10): Cloth construction and surface ornament should remain distinguishable.

## K13-08

**Clothing silhouette can depend on hidden support.** A garment's shape may depend on an underlayer, stiffening or a frame rather than the body's contour.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: equipment silhouette; Silhouette and garment layers; Mobility and equipment compatibility; Anatomy under clothing and equipment.
- Apply: Separate body, support layer and outer garment in a costume breakdown.
- Limit: A static costume shape does not establish comfort or range of motion.
- Check: Compare standing, sitting and reaching without silently changing the support structure.
- Constrains [K13-02](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-02): Equipment silhouette must agree with the actions the character performs.
- Supports [K13-14](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-14): A supported outer silhouette still needs a feasible way to put it on.

## K13-09

**A held pose and moving spacing carry different information.** Selecting key positions establishes poses; the intervals between positions determine how movement proceeds through time.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: timing; spacing; Pose selection and motion breakdown; Timing, spacing and arcs; Timing, pauses and purposeful stillness.
- Apply: Check contact, passing and recovery positions separately from their timing.
- Limit: Equal spacing does not produce every intended acceleration or weight impression.
- Check: Compare a slow and fast action with the same endpoints and inspect the intervening path.
- Requires [K03-01](ActionsAndCombat.md#k03-01): Presented acceleration should agree with the movement being represented.

## K13-10

**Surface detail cannot rescue a lost action silhouette.** Removing internal markings reveals whether contour and negative spaces distinguish a pose.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: recognition at play distance; Readability at gameplay distance; Silhouette readability; Silhouette, camera and motion checks.
- Apply: Compare action poses as small filled shapes before adding facial or costume detail.
- Limit: Some subtle acting requires internal facial information; silhouette is a selective check.
- Check: Ask which poses remain distinguishable at the intended camera distance.
- Requires [K10-07](VisualLanguage.md#k10-07): Silhouette review isolates boundary information from surface treatment.

## K13-11

**Maximum speed does not specify turning response.** In a steering model, limits on steering acceleration change how quickly velocity can turn even when maximum speed stays fixed.

- Kind: design inference. Focus: Timing, spacing and arcs; Cycles, variation and transitions; Individual movement signatures.
- Apply: Compare approach arcs and reversals when describing distinct movement signatures.
- Limit: Design inference: this models a trajectory, not whether a particular body can produce it.
- Check: Run identical target changes at equal top speed with different steering limits; inspect trajectories before choosing an animation interpretation.
- Requires [K01-03](Foundations.md#k01-03): Steering decisions and physical locomotion are separate layers.
- Requires [K13-01](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-01): The chosen trajectory still needs feasible body support and contacts.

## K13-12

**Articulated plate needs overlap and moving attachments.** Overlapping armor plates can move through joint-specific arrangements of straps and rivets; a sliding rivet in a slot permits travel that a fixed fastening would block.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Fit and articulated protection; Motion restriction, sound and interaction poses; deformation constraints; Deformation and range-of-motion constraints; Clipping and secondary chains.
- Apply: Specify overlap direction, attachment travel and clearance at the intended joint instead of drawing arbitrary plate bands.
- Limit: Historical arrangements vary; plate segmentation alone does not demonstrate a feasible movement range.
- Check: Trace flexion and extension while keeping each fastening attached; identify collisions or motion that requires a fixed rivet to slide.
- Requires [K13-01](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-01): Protection must accommodate the body's support and movement.
- Supports [K13-09](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-09): Key poses can expose attachment and clearance failures before interpolation.

## K13-13

**Task allocation can explain asymmetric armor.** Some lance equipment gives the two shoulders different protection and clearance because the arms perform different tasks.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Occupation and practiced movement; Grip, reach and leverage; Equipment silhouette and role recognition.
- Apply: Relate an asymmetric plate, opening or guard to grip, reach, coverage and the activity assigned to that side.
- Limit: This explains a particular design relationship, not a universal rule for armor or handedness.
- Check: Compare the two arm tasks and test both movement paths; remove any asymmetry that has no stated functional or expressive purpose.
- Requires [K13-12](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-12): When asymmetry changes joint protection, articulation must still work.
- Supports [K13-10](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-10): Task differences can be communicated through a readable action silhouette.

## K13-14

**Close-fitting protection needs a donning path.** A close helmet may need hinged openings to pass over the head; closures and supporting layers are part of how protection is worn.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Fit and articulated protection; Carrying, storage and attachment; Motion restriction, sound and interaction poses.
- Apply: Show how a fitted protective shell opens, attaches and is removed, including the room its closures need.
- Limit: Different equipment uses different closures; an underlayer's support role does not establish a universal material or thickness.
- Check: Trace entry and removal without passing the body through a closed shell; check that the wearer can reach the required closure.
- Supports [K13-08](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-08): Hidden layers can explain an outer silhouette and attachment.
- Constrains [K13-12](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-12): Donning closures and movement attachments must coexist without conflicting travel.

## K13-15

**A trot coordinates diagonal support pairs.** In a horse's trot, a forefoot and the opposite hindfoot support the body as a diagonal pair, alternating with the other pair. Faster trotting can include intervals without ground contact.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: locomotion; Gait, stride and foot contact; locomotion; Creature locomotion/contact diagrams.
- Apply: Build a contact timeline before changing stride length or speed; distinguish a diagonal trot from same-side pairing.
- Limit: This describes a particular gait, not every quadruped or speed; support duration and suspension must be checked for the animal and movement represented.
- Check: Track all four feet through a cycle and flag unintended same-side contacts or an airborne phase shown as planted support.
- Requires [K13-03](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-03): Pairing has to agree with planted contacts, weight transfer and body displacement.
- Supports [K14-04](Audiovisual.md#k14-04): Footstep sound and dust can follow the actual contact timeline rather than an unrelated repeating pulse.
