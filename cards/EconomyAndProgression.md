## K04-01

**Progress and rewards need different observations.** A balance judgement requires a stated goal; an increased reward number does not by itself establish mastery, useful choice or suitable challenge.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: skill versus numerical power; capability and access.
- Apply: Separate knowledge, execution skill, equipment power and access when describing progression; name which a reward changes.
- Limit: No numeric curve, XP rate or loot coefficient is approved by this general distinction.
- Check: At equal equipment, does a returning player make a better decision or only receive a larger number?
- Requires [K02-03](PlayerExperience.md#k02-03): Mastery claims require independent transfer rather than prompted success.
- Supports [K04-14](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-14): The value of another reward can be assessed separately from its delivered quantity.

## K04-02

**Resources connect producers and users.** Production, exchange and consumption connect resource availability to the actions that depend on it; an isolated price does not describe the whole economy.

- Kind: design inference. Focus: stocks and flows; crafting chains.
- Apply: Reuse the existing economy library to draw producers, stores, transfers and consumers before assigning prices or trading identities.
- Limit: A coherent resource flow is a model, not proof of long-term balance or an approved crafting system.
- Check: Trace one resource from acquisition to final use and identify who can permanently block that flow.
- Requires [K08-01](Societies.md#k08-01): Material life needs an account of supply, labour and recurring consumption.

## K04-03

**Balance compares strategies under conditions.** A dominant strategy and a difficult task are different problems; random outcomes add uncertainty to observed performance.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: dominant strategies; perceived fairness.
- Apply: Compare strategies across encounter conditions and record exposure counts alongside outcomes; reuse existing probability guidance for odds.
- Limit: One unlucky run, an equal pick rate or automatic difficulty adjustment does not establish fairness.
- Check: Name a condition in which each intended alternative is useful, then test whether one still wins everywhere.
- Requires [K16-03](ResearchAndProduction.md#k16-03): Strategy comparisons need repeatable conditions and a declared success measure.
- Requires [K04-07](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-07): Strategy comparison must include spread and tails rather than only average outcomes.

## K04-04

**Observe repeated cost and displaced play.** A repeated capacity or exhaustion task can create predictable travel and handling work without introducing a new inference.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: player behaviour metrics.
- Apply: For a recurring sink compare its first, fifth and later uses; retain it only if the desired choice remains observable.
- Limit: Removing all friction is not a universal goal; resource pressure may be intentional and needs testing.
- Check: Measure decisions and forced repeated actions separately over a repeated return loop.
- Constrains [K03-04](ActionsAndCombat.md#k03-04): Recovery pressure should not silently replace participation with repetitive handling.

## K04-05

**Opportunity cost is the best forgone alternative.** The cost of a choice includes its direct cost and the value of the next best mutually exclusive alternative.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: scarcity; pricing.
- Apply: Compare spending a resource now with its strongest plausible alternative use.
- Limit: Do not sum every rejected option or assume all players value them equally.
- Check: State what the chosen action prevents the player from doing next.
- Supports [K01-02](Foundations.md#k01-02): Meaningful alternatives require identifiable consequences.
- Supports [K02-02](PlayerExperience.md#k02-02): The presentation of an alternative can change how its forgone value is perceived.
- Supports [K04-10](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-10): Relative forgone output explains specialization beyond absolute productivity.

## K04-06

**Relative prices reshape incentives.** A change in one cost relative to another can alter which production or purchase option is attractive.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: conversion; currency and barter; pricing; repair costs.
- Apply: Compare repair, replacement and crafting using their relative resource and time costs.
- Limit: Scaling every price equally may leave choices unchanged while altering affordability.
- Check: Recalculate the preferred option after changing one cost at a time.
- Requires [K04-02](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-02): Conversions need identified producers and consumers.

## K04-07

**Distributions reveal what averages hide.** A mean alone does not describe spread, skew or rare extremes; empirical and analytic distributions play different roles.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: distributions; variance and streaks; loot tables.
- Apply: Compare completion and reward outcomes by quantiles and tails as well as means.
- Limit: A convenient distribution is a model, not evidence that actual outcomes follow it.
- Check: Identify who experiences the longest dry spells and compare them with the model.
- Requires [K16-01](ResearchAndProduction.md#k16-01): A balance claim needs an observable outcome distribution.
- Supports [K04-12](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-12): Comparing eligible and overall attempts can expose different reward frequencies.

## K04-08

**Accumulation is a stock and flow question.** A stored quantity changes through incoming and outgoing flows; sustained positive net flow accumulates stock.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: sources and sinks; renewal and depletion; runaway accumulation; persistent-world depletion.
- Apply: Separate reserve, replenishment, consumption and capacity over repeated visits.
- Limit: A fixed stock model does not establish a stable price or an infinite renewable supply.
- Check: Trace a resource through repeated production and use, including an unused surplus.
- Requires [K01-04](Foundations.md#k01-04): Accumulation must respect the approved world time contract.

## K04-09

**Equal expected value can hide different exposure.** Outcomes with similar averages can impose different frequencies and magnitudes of loss.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: variance and streaks; simulation scenarios; interpretation limits.
- Apply: Compare repeated sessions and worst plausible sequences alongside average payout.
- Limit: This statistical comparison does not establish what risk players enjoy.
- Check: Check whether a short-session participant encounters a materially different outcome than a frequent participant.
- Supports [K04-04](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-04): Repeated cost observation should include displaced play and extreme outcomes.
- Requires [K04-13](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-13): When comparing streaks, specify whether past outcomes change the next chance.

## K04-10

**Specialization depends on relative opportunity cost.** One producer can outperform another at every task while still facing a higher opportunity cost for a particular output.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: specialisation; crafting chains; NPC and player markets.
- Apply: Compare forgone alternatives before assigning production roles or evaluating gains from exchange.
- Limit: Potential gains require feasible exchange and suitable terms; absolute productivity alone does not establish shared benefit.
- Check: Construct a case where one participant is faster at both tasks and compare relative costs.
- Requires [K04-05](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-05): Comparative advantage is expressed through the best forgone alternative.
- Supports [K05-01](CooperativePlay.md#k05-01): Different relative costs can create a reason for interdependence.

## K04-11

**A best response depends on others' choices.** A best response maximizes a participant's payoff given others' strategies; mutual best responses form an equilibrium against unilateral deviation.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: dominant strategies; counters and niches.
- Apply: Compare strategies against several opponent or partner choices before calling one universally strongest.
- Limit: An equilibrium need not maximize collective benefit or predict actual player behavior.
- Check: Hold others' choices fixed, then test every unilateral alternative for an improvement.
- Supports [K04-03](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-03): Conditional comparisons distinguish strategic advantage from raw difficulty.
- Constrains [K05-09](CooperativePlay.md#k05-09): Stability does not establish a fair allocation.

## K04-12

**Conditional reward chances use eligible outcomes.** In a finite probability model, conditioning on an eligible event restricts the outcome space: P(reward given eligibility) = P(reward and eligibility) / P(eligibility), when the denominator is positive.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: conditional probability; loot tables.
- Apply: Separate the chance of reaching a reward table from the chance of receiving an item after reaching it.
- Limit: A condition with zero probability cannot use this ratio; eligibility changes may also change the underlying model.
- Check: Enumerate eligible outcomes and compare conditional item chances with the chance across all attempts.
- Supports [K04-07](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-07): Conditional distributions distinguish eligible attempts from the overall population.
- Requires [K16-01](ResearchAndProduction.md#k16-01): Eligibility and the counted outcome must be defined before measuring reward frequency.

## K04-13

**Independent rolls do not repay earlier misses.** For independent trials with a fixed success probability, earlier misses do not increase the next trial's success chance.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: variance and streaks; conditional probability; loot tables.
- Apply: Distinguish repeated independent loot rolls from drawing without replacement or changing probabilities after failure.
- Limit: A finite bag, adaptive reward rule or shared state can violate independence; this is not a fairness recommendation.
- Check: Compare the next-outcome distribution after different histories under the stated reward rule.
- Requires [K04-12](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-12): History-dependent chances must be evaluated on the relevant conditions.
- Constrains [K04-09](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-09): When comparing equal-mean rewards, an independence assumption excludes history-based compensation.

## K04-14

**Equal quantities can have unequal marginal value.** Under diminishing marginal utility, an additional unit of a consumed good contributes less value when more of that good has already been consumed, holding other goods constant.

- Kind: design inference. Focus: scarcity; reward timing.
- Apply: Compare the same reward at different consumption levels and times; distinguish the amount delivered from the additional benefit it can provide then.
- Limit: Design inference: diminishing marginal utility is a model condition, not a universal player response or a rule for collection, thresholds or complementary items.
- Check: Hold the reward amount fixed and compare its available uses before and after the relevant need has been met.
- Requires [K04-05](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-05): Judging the next unit requires comparing its benefit with the alternative forgone.
- Constrains [K04-01](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-01): Delivering another equal reward does not establish equal additional value or perceived progress.
