## K09-01

**World knowledge needs a viewpoint.** Interactive structure can establish atmosphere and foreshadow later events while teaching the actions needed to participate.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: tone; discovery order.
- Apply: Turn a lore claim into something a player can encounter, interpret and use; distinguish an inhabitant belief from an established world fact.
- Limit: An opening does not need a fixed prologue, and an evocative theme is not evidence of a playable consequence.
- Check: State what the player can infer now, what remains uncertain and which next action that supports.
- Requires [K01-02](Foundations.md#k01-02): Useful knowledge must reach the player before it is needed for a consequential choice.
- Supports [K09-14](Narrative.md#k09-14): An inhabitant's wording can help distinguish a personal perspective from a universal world claim.

## K09-02

**Dialogue is an action between participants.** Interactive scenes must accommodate what a player can do and what another participant knows or wants; fixed exposition alone does not define that interaction.

- Kind: design inference. Focus: desires; Speech acts, subtext and intention.
- Apply: Give a dialogue participant a current goal and a reason to reveal, withhold or reinterpret information; include refusal and interruption.
- Limit: This is a design deduction, not a claim that a game needs branching dialogue or a particular reputation system.
- Check: Read the exchange with its informational reward removed: does a conflict or relationship still change?
- Requires [K08-02](Societies.md#k08-02): Institutional roles and personal obligations explain why information matters to a speaker.
- Supports [K09-13](Narrative.md#k09-13): An interaction can reveal whether a claimed trait agrees with a consequential choice.

## K09-03

**Dependencies expose narrative bottlenecks.** A puzzle dependency diagram can reveal impossible requirements, narrow bottlenecks and early actions whose later necessity becomes irreversible.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: causality; branching; failure and continuation.
- Apply: Draw prerequisites for a quest, preserve a viable path after plausible refusal and distinguish optional order from distinct outcomes.
- Limit: More branches are not automatically better; a deliberate bottleneck still needs understandable conditions and a tested recovery path.
- Check: Trace completion without a missable early object and record the specific state where progress would stop.
- Supports [K03-02](ActionsAndCombat.md#k03-02): Fair inference requires accessible evidence and actionable prerequisites.
- Requires [K06-10](SpatialDesign.md#k06-10): A narrative prerequisite must survive its mapping into traversable space.

## K09-04

**A trace needs a recoverable causal sequence.** A coherent interaction can reuse a small set of objects in several roles; physical locations and events can jointly communicate a sequence.

- Kind: design inference. Focus: objects as evidence.
- Apply: Compose environmental storytelling as prior event, surviving trace and plausible player inference; allow the group to compare interpretations.
- Limit: A decorative arrangement is not proof that players infer its intended story, and ambiguity should not hide essential instructions.
- Check: Ask players to explain the likely event and point to the particular traces supporting their interpretation.
- Requires [K12-03](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-03): Dressing must preserve evidence of sequence and use instead of random debris.

## K09-05

**Causal unity differs from sharing a protagonist.** Events can involve the same person without forming a causal whole; removing a necessary event changes what follows.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Causality, conflict and stakes; Plot and story distinction; causality.
- Apply: Link a scene to the action or decision that makes the next scene possible.
- Limit: This is a dramatic construction method, not a requirement that all exploration be linear.
- Check: Remove a scene and identify the consequence that can no longer follow.
- Requires [K09-03](Narrative.md#k09-03): Causal dependencies should remain explicit even when discovery order varies.

## K09-06

**A reversal changes the meaning of an earlier action.** A dramatic reversal can turn an intended outcome into its opposite through the consequences already established.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: escalation; turning points; Setup, payoff, foreshadowing and reveal; Pacing, suspense, surprise and curiosity.
- Apply: Prepare the condition that makes a later reversal intelligible.
- Limit: An unexplained coincidence can surprise without supporting a causal interpretation.
- Check: After the reveal, identify the earlier action that enabled it.
- Requires [K09-04](Narrative.md#k09-04): A surprising outcome still needs a recoverable causal sequence.

## K09-07

**Recognition matters when knowledge changes action.** Moving from ignorance to knowledge can change relationships and the actions available to characters.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: secrets; Clues, inference and information gaps; relationship consequences; Relationship change, trust and betrayal.
- Apply: Describe what a discovery permits, prevents or reinterprets.
- Limit: A lore fact with no consequence can enrich context but does not automatically create a turning point.
- Check: Compare the character's plausible choice before and after learning the fact.
- Requires [K01-02](Foundations.md#k01-02): Changed knowledge must affect an identifiable choice or interpretation.

## K09-08

**Repeated descriptions should respect elapsed experience.** Text describing an initial surprise can contradict a later visit; stable descriptions and state-specific reactions serve different purposes.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Repetition, memory and narrative economy; Interruptions, repetition and return visits; revisiting changed places.
- Apply: Distinguish enduring properties, first discovery and changed conditions.
- Limit: Repeating less text must not remove information needed after an interruption.
- Check: Read first arrival and return text against the same retained state.
- Requires [K01-04](Foundations.md#k01-04): Presentation of a return visit must agree with persistent change.

## K09-09

**Narrative voice and access to experience are separate choices.** Who tells a story differs from whose perspective it adopts; third-person narration can still be restricted to one character's experience.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Voice, behavior and physical characterization; voice; NPC knowledge, memory and deception.
- Apply: State who speaks, whose inner experience is available and which events remain inaccessible.
- Limit: Grammatical person alone does not determine knowledge or perspective.
- Check: Mark every assertion that exceeds the chosen viewpoint and justify the access or remove it.
- Constrains [K09-02](Narrative.md#k09-02): A speaker's available information limits what a conversation can plausibly reveal.
- Supports [K05-03](CooperativePlay.md#k05-03): Different information access can explain divergent participant accounts.

## K09-10

**An unreliable account needs interpretable disagreement.** A narrator's account can conflict with what the audience can infer from the wider narrative.

- Kind: design inference. Focus: reliable and unreliable accounts; Reliability and unreliable accounts; Clues, inference and information gaps; subtext; NPC knowledge, memory and deception.
- Apply: Preserve evidence that lets a reader question a witness's interpretation without making every fact arbitrary.
- Limit: Design inference: unreliability requires an interpretable discrepancy; obscurity alone does not establish it.
- Check: Identify the contradictory evidence and compare at least two explanations for the account.
- Requires [K09-09](Narrative.md#k09-09): The account's knowledge and perspective must be distinguishable.
- Supports [K12-12](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-12): Material traces can constrain an account's proposed event order.
- Supports [K03-08](ActionsAndCombat.md#k03-08): Inspection can supply evidence without directly announcing the conclusion.
- Requires [K09-12](Narrative.md#k09-12): When a contradiction depends on timing, compare event order with disclosure order.

## K09-11

**Suspense and surprise need different information.** Suspense involves anticipating an uncertain outcome; surprise depends on an event conflicting with what the audience expected.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Setup, payoff, foreshadowing and reveal; Pacing, suspense, surprise and curiosity; Player, protagonist and narrator separation; stakes.
- Apply: State what the player knows before a reveal and which unresolved consequence they can anticipate.
- Limit: Concealment alone does not create suspense; anticipation and surprise may occur in different phases of the same sequence.
- Check: List the audience's available clues before the event; identify the prediction or unresolved outcome those clues support.
- Requires [K09-09](Narrative.md#k09-09): Audience access to information must be separated from narrative voice.
- Requires [K02-01](PlayerExperience.md#k02-01): An earlier clue must remain retrievable when the later consequence depends on it.

## K09-12

**Event chronology differs from disclosure order.** Events can be disclosed in reverse chronological order or through other arrangements that differ from when they occurred.

- Kind: design inference. Focus: Plot and story distinction; discovery order; Clues, inference and information gaps; Narrative continuity across exploration order; missable discovery.
- Apply: Keep a causal timeline alongside the player's discovery sequence; finding a ruin before its destruction is described should revise interpretation without reversing causation.
- Limit: Design inference: an exploration route must not silently supply knowledge that the player could have missed.
- Check: Trace two permitted discovery orders and identify which causal links each can infer without inaccessible evidence.
- Requires [K09-05](Narrative.md#k09-05): Rearranging disclosure still needs an intelligible causal structure.
- Supports [K09-11](Narrative.md#k09-11): Changing available information changes whether a moment invites anticipation or surprise.
- Requires [K06-10](SpatialDesign.md#k06-10): When evidence is spatially gated, information prerequisites must survive room connectivity.

## K09-13

**Actions can contradict an asserted character trait.** A narrative can characterize someone through statements, speech and conduct; what the person repeatedly does can undermine a trait asserted by that person or by a narrator.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Motivation and credible action; Agency, constraints and contradiction; Voice, behavior and physical characterization.
- Apply: For a claimed motive or value, identify a consequential choice that supports or challenges it and explain whether the discrepancy is intentional.
- Limit: One surprising act does not establish a fixed personality; deception, competing obligations or character change can explain a discrepancy.
- Check: Remove the trait label and inspect the available choices and actions; ask what characterization remains supported by evidence.
- Requires [K09-02](Narrative.md#k09-02): A consequential interaction needs the participant's current goal and available actions.
- Supports [K09-10](Narrative.md#k09-10): Disagreement between asserted traits and observed conduct can supply interpretable evidence for questioning an account.

## K09-14

**Distinctive speech need not transcribe every sound.** Vocabulary, recurring expressions and manner of speaking can individualize a voice without respelling every pronounced sound.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Voice, behavior and physical characterization; Distinct voices and register; readability.
- Apply: Distinguish speakers through what they notice, how they frame a request and which words fit their current situation.
- Limit: A profession or cultural identity does not determine one uniform voice; heavy phonetic spelling can obstruct understanding and flatten a person into a caricature.
- Check: Remove names from a short exchange and compare speaker recognition with comprehension; identify which wording carries the difference.
- Requires [K09-13](Narrative.md#k09-13): A distinctive voice still needs a meaningful relationship to the character's actions and situation.
- Requires [K08-04](Societies.md#k08-04): Individual differences within a community must survive its shared cultural label.
