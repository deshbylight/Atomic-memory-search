## K12-16

**Enamel compartments can be added or cut into a base.** Glass-like enamel can fill cells formed by added metal partitions or recesses cut into a metal base; similar face patterns can hide different construction.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: construction; Resources and production technology; Glass, ceramic, bone and organic materials; Transitions, seams and layering.
- Apply: Choose the compartment construction before drawing a cutaway, exposed rim or damaged prop variant.
- Limit: A face view alone may not distinguish the methods; decorative linework is insufficient evidence of internal construction.
- Check: Trace one compartment in section: identify its floor and whether its walls were added or left from the base.
- Supports [K12-03](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-03): A shared compartment-making method can unify an object family beyond a repeated painted motif.
- Supports [K12-15](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-15): A visible facing and its supporting structure must be distinguished before exposing their section.

## K12-17

**Transparent enamel thickness can produce color variation.** Transparent enamel over engraved low relief can be thicker in deeper recesses and appear darker there despite using one tint.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: surface layers; Glass, ceramic, bone and organic materials; Roughness, metallicity and translucency.
- Apply: Separate substrate relief, transparent coating and perceived color before adding a painted gradient to a decorative panel.
- Limit: The outcome depends on coating and illumination; this example supplies no universal thickness-to-color formula.
- Check: Compare flat and relieved substrates with the same proposed tint, and check whether the shaded pattern follows the layer section.
- Supports [K10-10](VisualLanguage.md#k10-10): Apparent surface color can change with relief rather than a change of pigment.
- Requires [K12-16](MaterialCraft.md#k12-16): The filled recess and its support must be defined before attributing darkness to coating thickness.

## K12-18

**Transparent enamel needs an open light path to transmit from behind.** Transparent enamel held in a metal framework without continuous metal backing can admit light through its colored cells.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: surface layers; Glass, ceramic, bone and organic materials; Roughness, metallicity and translucency.
- Apply: Distinguish an open-backed colored insert from one on an opaque plate when drawing front-lit and backlit prop views.
- Limit: A transparent insert is not self-luminous; opaque backing blocks the proposed through-light path.
- Check: Trace the backlight through the section; compare with the same insert backed by an opaque plate and reject unexplained glow.
- Requires [K12-16](MaterialCraft.md#k12-16): Cell boundaries do not by themselves specify whether a continuous opaque base remains behind the enamel.
- Requires [K10-13](VisualLanguage.md#k10-13): A claimed backlit appearance needs a light direction consistent with the object section.

## K12-19

**Fresnel reflection and transmission depend on interface angle.** At a smooth dielectric boundary, the reflected and transmitted proportions depend on incidence angle and the refractive indices on both sides.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Glass, ceramic, bone and organic materials; Roughness, metallicity and translucency.
- Apply: Compare frontal and oblique views of a glazed insert under the same environment before assigning it one constant transparent appearance.
- Limit: Surface roughness and surrounding light also matter; angle dependence alone supplies neither a universal opacity nor a complete material description.
- Check: Rotate the view while retaining the object and illumination; check whether the proposed reflected and transmitted features change coherently.
- Requires [K10-02](VisualLanguage.md#k10-02): Surface orientation and roughness must be separated from the underlying value hierarchy.
- Constrains [K12-18](MaterialCraft.md#k12-18): An open backing permits a light path but does not make a colored insert equally transparent at every angle.

## K12-20

**Refraction depends on both media at a boundary.** At an ideal specular boundary, the transmitted direction depends on the incident direction, surface normal and ratio of the refractive indices of the two media.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: material identity; Glass, ceramic, bone and organic materials.
- Apply: State the surrounding medium and trace entry and exit surfaces before drawing a displaced image through a clear object.
- Limit: A material label alone does not determine the visible displacement; shape, viewing direction and each crossed boundary matter.
- Check: Trace one background feature through the proposed section and check whether its apparent direction agrees with the entry and exit boundaries.
- Supports [K12-18](MaterialCraft.md#k12-18): A continuous through-light path still needs its direction traced across each material boundary.
- Requires [K10-03](VisualLanguage.md#k10-03): The intended viewpoint fixes which boundaries and transmitted directions the observer sees.

## K12-21

**Total internal reflection needs the appropriate interface direction.** When light travels from higher to lower refractive index, incidence beyond the critical angle produces total internal reflection at an ideal dielectric interface.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Glass, ceramic, bone and organic materials; Roughness, metallicity and translucency.
- Apply: Distinguish viewing a water surface from below from viewing it from above before depicting the surface as a transmitting window or a mirror.
- Limit: This is not a rule that every steep view of transparent material becomes opaque; the media order and internal incidence angle matter.
- Check: Label both media and the incident side on a section; reject a mirror transition justified only by the exterior camera angle.
- Requires [K12-20](MaterialCraft.md#k12-20): The direction of travel and refractive-index ratio must be established before testing the critical-angle condition.
- Constrains [K12-19](MaterialCraft.md#k12-19): A transmitting proportion cannot remain positive beyond the ideal interface condition for total internal reflection.
