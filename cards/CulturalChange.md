## K08-16

**Stated values do not determine everyday conduct.** Values describe desirable states and norms prescribe conduct, but everyday behavior can depart from either.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: values and norms; Avoiding universalized stereotypes.
- Apply: Describe a declared obligation, an ordinary choice and the explanation participants give for the gap.
- Limit: One observed violation does not establish that nobody holds the value.
- Check: Compare a public statement with conduct in two situations before assigning a uniform cultural trait.
- Requires [K08-04](Societies.md#k08-04): Inhabitants can share membership while acting differently.
- Supports [K05-11](CooperativePlay.md#k05-11): Public agreement need not establish private endorsement.

## K08-17

**Social sanctions need not be legal penalties.** Norms can be sustained by informal approval, ridicule or exclusion as well as written rules; formality and severity are separate questions.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: law and conflict; Law, custom, courts and punishment; values and norms.
- Apply: Name who responds to a breach, what they do and which rule they invoke.
- Limit: A written prohibition does not establish uniform enforcement.
- Check: Trace the same breach before peers and an official; distinguish their authority and consequences.
- Requires [K08-16](CulturalChange.md#k08-16): Expected conduct must be distinguished from observed behavior.
- Requires [K08-07](Societies.md#k08-07): An official response depends on the recognized scope of authority.

## K08-18

**Distinct subgroup identity need not imply rebellion.** A subculture can share much of its parent culture; a counterculture explicitly challenges some dominant norms or values.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Faction interests and interdependence; variation within a society; Variation within cultures.
- Apply: Name what a faction retains and what it contests before assigning political opposition.
- Limit: Distinct clothing or a shared pastime alone does not establish rejection of the wider society.
- Check: Describe a disagreement within the subgroup and an institution it still shares with neighbors.
- Requires [K08-04](Societies.md#k08-04): Internal variation remains relevant within a named subgroup.
- Supports [K08-16](CulturalChange.md#k08-16): Declarations and everyday participation can reveal different commitments.

## K08-19

**Cultural contact can change a borrowed form.** Cultural forms can spread through contact and acquire new uses or meanings; exchange can produce hybrid practices rather than simple replacement.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Continuity, reuse and cultural layering; Culture as learned and changing practice; Migration, diaspora and cultural contact.
- Apply: Trace who carries a practice, who adopts it and what local use changes.
- Limit: A shared object form alone does not establish a migration route or identical beliefs.
- Check: Compare use before and after contact; retain a plausible carrier and evidence of local reinterpretation.
- Supports [K08-18](CulturalChange.md#k08-18): Borrowed practices can coexist with a distinct subgroup identity.
- Supports [K08-03](Societies.md#k08-03): Material traces can record changed use without wholesale replacement.
