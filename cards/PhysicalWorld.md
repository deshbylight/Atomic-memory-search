## K07-01

**Water storage is not water throughput.** Groundwater occupies connected pores and fractures; porosity describes void space, while water movement also depends on connectivity and pressure differences.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Groundwater, springs and aquifers.
- Apply: For a wet underground location identify entry, storage and exit paths before adding pools, seepage or settlement water supply.
- Limit: A porous-looking surface alone does not establish a fast drain, and greater depth does not imply a larger open lake.
- Check: Trace where incoming water accumulates and how it could leave under the assumed conditions.
- Constrains [K11-03](BuiltWorld.md#k11-03): Underground occupation and maintenance depend on the water paths through the structure.
- Supports [K07-07](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-07): Water paths expose minerals to the conditions that can produce selective dissolution.
- Supports [K07-13](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-13): Water paths can explain channel form through transport and deposition.

## K07-02

**Ecology includes dependencies and habitat makers.** Mutualists, parasites and habitat-forming organisms connect populations; a species can alter community structure beyond its own abundance.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: symbiosis and competition.
- Apply: Give a proposed creature one resource dependency and one effect on another organism or habitat instead of adding an isolated species list.
- Limit: A designed dependency is not a fixed population law or evidence that a particular species is indispensable.
- Check: Remove the proposed organism in a thought experiment and identify which dependent activity or shelter changes.
- Supports [K13-01](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-01): Creature form and behaviour should be compatible with its proposed resource and habitat relationships.
- Supports [K07-15](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-15): Visible fungal growth needs substrate relationships that sustain the hidden feeding structure.

## K07-03

**Vegetation has layers and limiting conditions.** Climate, water availability and disturbance influence vegetation; canopy layers change the habitats and light conditions beneath them.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: growth conditions; spatial gradients.
- Apply: Choose a limiting condition and vegetation layers before composing a biome boundary; show a transition rather than an unexplained texture swap.
- Limit: A biome label does not determine every local organism, and surface forest models do not automatically apply to an unlit dungeon.
- Check: Explain a patch of growth, a bare patch and a transition through their local conditions.
- Constrains [K12-02](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-02): Natural dressing should follow conditions that can be read at the scale of the scene.

## K07-04

**Disturbance and fantasy need explicit timescales.** Fire, grazing and other disturbances can maintain or alter a community; recovery depends on surviving organisms and local conditions.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: recovery; declared physical exceptions.
- Apply: For a fantasy departure name the ordinary dependency being replaced, its replacement and what traces or limits remain.
- Limit: The replacement is a proposal until approved and modelled; biology does not justify arbitrary fast regrowth or offline simulation.
- Check: Compare immediately after disturbance, after active-world recovery time and after a closed-world pause.
- Constrains [K01-04](Foundations.md#k01-04): Ecological recovery must respect the world's clock and persistence rules.

## K07-05

**Freeze-thaw damage requires water and temperature cycling.** Water entering fractures can expand on freezing; repeated thawing allows further penetration and loosening.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Weathering, erosion and sediment transport; collapse and flood.
- Apply: Relate frost-broken faces and accumulated fragments to wet exposed cracks.
- Limit: Persistent cold, warmth or dryness limits this particular process.
- Check: Name where water enters and when the proposed exposure crosses freezing.
- Supports [K12-02](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-02): Damage distribution needs a local cause and scale.

## K07-06

**Roots enlarge existing weaknesses.** Growing roots can widen fractures; burrowing animals can remove soil and expose rock to other weathering.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: roots and structural damage; Slope instability and collapse.
- Apply: Distinguish root access, fracture widening and later fragment release.
- Limit: A root intersecting pristine massive rock does not explain how it established there.
- Check: Trace a plausible initial opening and subsequent growth path.
- Requires [K07-03](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-03): Vegetation still needs suitable growth conditions.

## K07-07

**Chemical decay depends on the mineral.** Weakly acidic water can dissolve calcite, while other minerals weather into different solids and dissolved ions.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Minerals and rock families; Karst and cave formation; Water quality and chemistry.
- Apply: Choose a rock family before describing cavities, residual bands or mineral staining.
- Limit: Not every cave is dissolved limestone and not every wet surface corrodes alike.
- Check: Explain why adjacent material survives or disappears under the same exposure.
- Supports [K12-02](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-02): Material-specific weathering produces structured surface variation.
- Supports [K12-06](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-06): Mineral-dependent dissolution explains which material is selectively removed and which survives.

## K07-08

**Wetness changes slope strength nonlinearly.** Moist granular material can gain cohesion from surface tension; saturation and pore pressure can reduce friction and stability.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Slope instability and collapse; Infiltration, drainage and erosion; Hazard exposure and adaptation.
- Apply: Connect slope failure to material, water state and a trigger.
- Limit: This supports fictional plausibility, not an engineering safety assessment.
- Check: Compare the proposed slope when dry, moist and saturated.
- Constrains [K11-03](BuiltWorld.md#k11-03): Support and drainage cannot be evaluated independently of ground conditions.

## K07-09

**Nutrients and energy impose different dependencies.** Nutrients move between organisms and nonliving reservoirs; weathering, decomposition and transport affect local availability.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Nutrient cycles and decomposition; resource gradients; Settlement pollution and waste.
- Apply: Trace a nutrient input, storage reservoir and return path alongside an energy source.
- Limit: Recycling matter does not provide unlimited usable energy.
- Check: Identify what enters an isolated habitat and what leaves or accumulates.
- Requires [K07-02](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-02): Ecological dependencies include nonliving reservoirs.

## K07-10

**Nutrient enrichment can deplete oxygen.** Extra nutrients can increase algal growth; subsequent decay can consume dissolved oxygen and harm aquatic organisms.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Salinity and contamination; Settlement pollution and waste.
- Apply: Connect an enriched water body to upstream waste or runoff and downstream consequences.
- Limit: Green water alone does not establish this cause or toxicity.
- Check: Trace nutrient input, biological growth and oxygen demand as separate stages.
- Requires [K07-01](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-01): Water throughput determines where an input is transported.

## K07-11

**Carrying capacity varies with conditions.** Available resources and seasonal or disturbance conditions change the population an environment can support.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: population dynamics; Population dynamics and carrying capacity; carrying capacity.
- Apply: Compare a habitat before and after a limiting resource changes.
- Limit: A logistic curve with constant capacity is a simplification, not a universal prediction.
- Check: Identify which resource becomes limiting in the least favorable season.
- Constrains [K13-01](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-01): A proposed creature population needs resources for its activity.

## K07-12

**Starting erosion differs from remaining in suspension.** Flow needed to entrain a particle can differ from flow needed to keep it suspended; cohesive fine sediment can resist initial removal.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: erosion; soil and sediment; Weathering, erosion and sediment transport.
- Apply: Distinguish a deposit being scoured from material already carried by the water.
- Limit: Particle size alone is insufficient; cohesion and local flow conditions matter.
- Check: Explain both how a deposit was first mobilized and where it could later settle.
- Requires [K07-01](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-01): Local water paths establish the relevant flow conditions.
- Supports [K12-11](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-11): Selective transport can produce spatially sorted deposits.

## K07-13

**Channel form reflects transport and confinement.** Braided channels divide around sediment bars when supply and transport favor deposition; a meandering channel can migrate through outer-bank erosion and inner-bank deposition.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: landforms; Mountain, plateau, basin and valley systems; Watersheds and drainage networks; Rivers, lakes, wetlands and floods.
- Apply: Relate channel pattern to sediment supply, valley confinement and local bank conditions.
- Limit: These patterns are not compulsory stages through which every river progresses.
- Check: For a proposed bend, locate erosion and deposition; for braided reaches, explain the sediment bars.
- Requires [K07-12](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-12): Channel change depends on transport and deposition rather than a decorative river outline.
- Supports [K12-11](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-11): Bank and floodplain deposits should agree with the proposed channel processes.

## K07-14

**Relative ages do not give elapsed durations.** A feature that cuts another is younger than what it cuts; undisturbed sediment layers usually place younger deposits above older ones.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: geology; Geological and biological time; Stratigraphy and deposition; Legible environmental traces of historical events.
- Apply: Build a relative event sequence from contacts and intersections before assigning dates.
- Limit: Overturning and disturbance can alter layer order; relative ordering alone gives no duration.
- Check: Mark the observed contact that supports each ordering and identify any missing interval.
- Supports [K12-12](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-12): Overlapping alterations can communicate an analogous order in constructed surfaces.
- Supports [K09-04](Narrative.md#k09-04): An event sequence needs surviving evidence rather than an unsupported timestamp.

## K07-15

**A visible fungus can hide its feeding network.** In a wood-decay fungus, a visible bracket is a reproductive structure, while a branching mycelium feeds within the substrate on organic material.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Nutrient cycles and decomposition; Plant, animal, fungal and microbial life; fungal ecology.
- Apply: Connect visible fungal growth to colonized material and a food supply instead of treating scattered caps as the entire organism.
- Limit: Not every fungus forms a mushroom; visible fruiting bodies do not measure the full extent of a feeding network.
- Check: Trace a fruiting cluster to an available organic substrate and distinguish visible reproduction from the hidden feeding structure.
- Requires [K07-09](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-09): Decomposition must connect growth to a material and nutrient source.
- Supports [K12-02](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-02): Substrate and colonization can explain localized growth instead of uniform surface decoration.
