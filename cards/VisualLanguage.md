## K10-01

**Hierarchy depends on relations.** A composition organizes a principal element and subordinate masses through size, grouping, intervals and surrounding negative space.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: shape hierarchy; focal hierarchy.
- Apply: Design a focal silhouette and large value masses before distributing detail; preserve that hierarchy in the player view.
- Limit: No shape has a universal moral character and no fixed ratio guarantees a successful composition.
- Check: Reduce the concept to a thumbnail and identify the intended first and second readings.
- Supports [K12-01](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-01): Environmental massing should preserve the chosen hierarchy at actual viewing distances.
- Supports [K10-15](VisualLanguage.md#k10-15): Intervals between repeated masses can create a competing background pattern.

## K10-02

**Value and material are independent controls.** Value relationships organize visible masses; roughness describes statistical surface orientation and changes angular reflection rather than merely changing base colour.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: value grouping; surface response.
- Apply: Separate value hierarchy from material identity when comparing lighting and surface variants.
- Limit: A grayscale thumbnail cannot validate material appearance, and a shiny patch does not by itself identify a physical material.
- Check: Compare a simplified value image and a lit material view; name the different question each answers.
- Supports [K12-02](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-02): Surface variation should reflect both material response and environmental conditions.

## K10-03

**Composition is viewpoint dependent.** The apparent balance of a picture changes with framing and attention; a small meaningful figure can outweigh a larger low-interest mass.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: camera-dependent readability; information priority.
- Apply: Judge scale cues and focal relationships from the playable camera, including nearby obstructions and movement.
- Limit: A balanced promotional frame does not establish readability during first-person traversal or group combat.
- Check: Compare approach, interaction and retreat views without moving objects solely to save one screenshot.
- Constrains [K14-03](Audiovisual.md#k14-03): Camera presentation must preserve the information needed for player control.

## K10-04

**Style is a chosen family of relationships.** Unity and variation act together; repetition alone does not create a compelling composition, while unrelated variation can dissolve hierarchy.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: visual grammar; comparative variants.
- Apply: Define a small grammar for proportion, edges, value grouping and ornament, then use controlled exceptions to mark meaningful differences.
- Limit: This proposes a method for comparing styles; it does not choose an art direction.
- Check: Show two different functions built with the same grammar and one exception with an explicit reason.
- Requires [K16-02](ResearchAndProduction.md#k16-02): Compare style candidates cheaply before committing to a large asset family.
- Requires [K10-08](VisualLanguage.md#k10-08): A motif family must adapt to different containing surfaces.

## K10-05

**Hue, value and chroma are separate controls.** A hue name alone leaves lightness and chromatic strength unspecified.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: palette; saturation hierarchy; Hue, value and chroma; Silhouette, grayscale and palette checks.
- Apply: Describe palettes using hue families, value ranges and relative chroma.
- Limit: A historical notation is not a display calibration method or an emotion formula.
- Check: Compare variants that change one dimension while holding the others approximately fixed.
- Supports [K10-02](VisualLanguage.md#k10-02): Value should remain independent from material and hue decisions.

## K10-06

**Available chroma is not equal across hues and values.** Pigment colors do not occupy a uniform sphere of equally available chroma at every hue and value.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Hue, value and chroma; Palette families and colour proportions.
- Apply: Check whether a proposed palette preserves its intended hierarchy in the actual medium.
- Limit: Paint behavior does not directly specify a digital display's gamut.
- Check: Compare the rendered swatches rather than assuming equal numeric saturation is perceptually equal.
- Requires [K12-04](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-04): Acceptance depends on the integrated presentation medium.

## K10-07

**Silhouette isolates boundary information.** A filled silhouette removes internal markings and shading, exposing the contour and negative spaces.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: recognition; Silhouette and recognition; Negative space and contour rhythm; Communicating form without surface detail.
- Apply: Compare recognition using the outer shape before relying on fine detail.
- Limit: Silhouette alone does not communicate every material, action or identity distinction.
- Check: Reduce the asset to a small solid shape at its intended viewing angle.
- Supports [K13-02](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-02): Equipment and body proportions should preserve useful action distinctions.

## K10-08

**A motif must fit its containing surface.** Repeating borders, panels and fills relate their internal shapes to a boundary and to intervals between repetitions.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: motif families; Motif transformation across asset classes; Ornament related to construction.
- Apply: Transform a motif to suit long borders, compact panels and wrapped surfaces.
- Limit: A surface ornament principle does not imply that every object shares an identical outline.
- Check: Compare the same motif at a corner, seam and curved boundary.
- Supports [K12-03](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-03): Related objects can share craft logic without identical decoration.
- Supports [K13-07](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-07): A costume motif must adapt to garment seams, folds and areas of cloth fullness.

## K10-09

**Eye height establishes the horizon for a level view.** In conventional level-view perspective the horizon corresponds to the observer's eye height; changing viewpoint changes visible surfaces.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: linear perspective; Horizon and eye height; Scale anchors and comparative size; Projection consistency across concept views.
- Apply: Include an eye-height or scale reference when comparing concept views.
- Limit: Tilting the camera and uneven ground require additional projection reasoning.
- Check: Check whether repeated equal-height objects agree with the stated viewpoint and ground plane.
- Constrains [K14-03](Audiovisual.md#k14-03): Framing affects the spatial information available to the player.

## K10-10

**Surface relief changes apparent color.** Ridges, fibers and surface orientation create local light and shadow even when their underlying dye is unchanged.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Local colour, light colour and material response; Local value versus illumination; Texture frequency and scale; Material-specific marks.
- Apply: Separate material color from the pattern created by weave, folds or relief.
- Limit: This observation does not specify a shader or a fixed lighting setup.
- Check: Compare the same surface under changed light direction before recoloring it.
- Requires [K12-02](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-02): Surface variation needs a material and viewing explanation.

## K10-11

**A transition can relate opposing directions.** An intermediate line, curve or branching form can bridge two opposed directions in a composition.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Point, line, plane and volume; Straight, curved and angular forms; Grouping, proximity and continuity; Ornament related to construction.
- Apply: Compare a direct angular junction with alternative transitions in a panel or ornamental support.
- Limit: This is a compositional device, not proof of structural support or universal aesthetic preference.
- Check: Compare small line studies and check whether the transition preserves the intended hierarchy.
- Requires [K10-08](VisualLanguage.md#k10-08): The transition must fit the surface or boundary that contains it.
- Requires [K11-05](BuiltWorld.md#k11-05): A visually connecting form needs an independent structural explanation if it carries load.

## K10-12

**Two flat values expose mass relationships.** A two-value study reduces a composition to contrasting flat masses; alternative assignments of dark and light can change its organization without changing outlines.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: value grouping; Value grouping and thumbnail reads; Detail distribution and visual rest.
- Apply: Compare several mass arrangements before adding modeling and surface detail.
- Limit: The study simplifies light and material; success in two values does not establish the final lighting.
- Check: Reduce the image and compare whether intended focal and resting areas remain distinct.
- Requires [K10-01](VisualLanguage.md#k10-01): Mass arrangements need a stated principal and subordinate relationship.
- Supports [K10-02](VisualLanguage.md#k10-02): Flat value studies isolate mass organization from material-dependent reflection.
- Supports [K10-13](VisualLanguage.md#k10-13): Light positions can be compared through the mass arrangements they produce.

## K10-13

**Light direction reorganizes visible form.** Moving a dominant light relative to the subject and observer changes the arrangement of light and shade; frontal lighting can reduce modeling contrasts visible under side lighting.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: light direction; shadow; Key, fill, rim and motivated light; Communicating form without surface detail.
- Apply: Compare side, front and overhead illumination. A light near the viewer can flatten the apparent shape even though geometry has not changed.
- Limit: Stylized shading and other light sources can alter the result; no direction guarantees readability for every shape.
- Check: Compare the object's visible planes under a side light and a light near the viewer; explain any lost shape definition before adding texture.
- Supports [K10-12](VisualLanguage.md#k10-12): Flat value studies can compare the changed light and shadow masses.
- Requires [K10-03](VisualLanguage.md#k10-03): Light direction must be evaluated relative to the viewing position.

## K10-14

**Several lights can fill each other's shadows.** Light arriving from several directions can illuminate areas shadowed from another source and complicate a single light-and-shade arrangement.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: shadow; Key, fill, rim and motivated light; Contrast allocation and dynamic range.
- Apply: Identify a dominant source and inspect how additional sources change the shadow masses supporting recognition.
- Limit: A complex light arrangement may be intentional; a dark shape is not automatically an unlit surface.
- Check: Compare each source alone with their combination; identify which added illumination erases a useful separation.
- Requires [K10-13](VisualLanguage.md#k10-13): Each source's direction must be related to the form and observer.
- Constrains [K10-01](VisualLanguage.md#k10-01): Competing illumination can weaken the intended information hierarchy.

## K10-15

**Repeated shapes also repeat the gaps between them.** Repeating a motif creates relationships between neighboring units and a secondary pattern in the background spaces; changing the interval changes both patterns.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Pattern, repetition and variation; negative space; Negative space and contour rhythm.
- Apply: Compare silhouettes of repeated supports or ornaments and separately inspect the negative spaces between them.
- Limit: This composition principle does not prescribe equal spacing, a fixed ratio or a universally pleasing pattern.
- Check: Hold the motif fixed, vary its spacing and compare which background shapes become dominant or accidentally close.
- Requires [K10-08](VisualLanguage.md#k10-08): The repeated unit and its gaps must fit the containing surface.
- Supports [K02-15](PlayerExperience.md#k02-15): Changed spacing can alter whether repetitions read as one group or separate clusters.
