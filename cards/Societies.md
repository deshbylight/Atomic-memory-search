## K08-01

**Tools and subsistence shape each other.** Technology, everyday practice and social organization influence each other; no single tool completely determines a culture.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Tools, materials and technology; supply chains.
- Apply: Describe an essential material, its workers, transport and users, then show how those activities modify space and objects.
- Limit: Avoid deriving an entire society from one resource or treating a technological change as an inevitable social outcome.
- Check: Can a settlement obtain its essentials and dispose of recurring by-products through the proposed routes?
- Supports [K04-02](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-02): Supply and consumption give an economy material participants and constraints.

## K08-02

**Institutions distribute meaning and authority.** Shared symbols, practices and communication can coordinate groups, but the same sign can carry different meanings for different participants.

- Kind: design inference. Focus: Authority and legitimacy; symbols and memory.
- Apply: For an institution identify an activity, a beneficiary, an obligation and a visible claim to authority; include internal disagreement.
- Limit: A religious emblem or rank symbol alone does not establish a uniform belief or an entire social hierarchy.
- Check: Explain how two inhabitants interpret the same ritual object differently and what each does about it.
- Supports [K09-02](Narrative.md#k09-02): Characters and relationships make institutional disagreements actionable.
- Requires [K08-07](Societies.md#k08-07): Institutional authority needs an identified scope and basis of acceptance.

## K08-03

**Material culture records use and change.** Objects and technologies participate in changing social practices; inherited forms can acquire new uses rather than simply disappear.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: repair and reuse; Continuity, reuse and cultural layering.
- Apply: Create an object family with a construction method, an everyday use and a later adaptation visible in repairs or replacements.
- Limit: Age does not mean universal decay; distinguish continued maintenance, abandonment and deliberate reuse.
- Check: Identify which visible feature was made originally, worn in use or added by a later occupant.
- Supports [K11-04](BuiltWorld.md#k11-04): Phases of occupation explain repairs and ruins better than uniform age effects.

## K08-04

**Settlement life is more than a cultural label.** A culture contains interrelated and varied practices; reducing inhabitants to one trait removes differences in roles and perspectives.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: shared and private space; variation within a society.
- Apply: Describe ordinary work, access to services and at least two perspectives within a settlement before assigning decorative identity.
- Limit: Do not map a real population to a fantasy moral category or present one imagined inhabitant as representing everyone.
- Check: Can two inhabitants disagree for material or social reasons without either ceasing to belong to the place?
- Supports [K11-02](BuiltWorld.md#k11-02): Service access and circulation expose differences in how inhabitants use a settlement.

## K08-05

**Familiar practices can carry unfamiliar goals.** A familiar activity can have different locally learned meanings, rules and preferred outcomes.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: cultural specificity; Insider and outsider perspectives; Avoiding universalized stereotypes; Work, leisure, games and festivals.
- Apply: Describe what participants consider success before borrowing an activity's outward form.
- Limit: One observed practice does not describe every member of a culture.
- Check: Show how an outsider's reasonable interpretation could differ from a participant's.
- Constrains [K05-01](CooperativePlay.md#k05-01): Shared activity requires checking whether its participants share the same goal.
- Supports [K08-10](Societies.md#k08-10): Food practices expose local meanings beyond physical availability.
- Supports [K08-12](Societies.md#k08-12): Locally accepted gift obligations can distinguish apparently similar exchanges.

## K08-06

**Belief becomes visible through practice.** Religious life can involve obligations, rituals, places and relationships beyond statements about deities.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: ritual; Ritual, devotion and moral practice; Sacred places, objects and pilgrimage; Taboos, purity and obligations.
- Apply: Specify who performs a practice, where, with what objects and obligations.
- Limit: No single definition or practice represents all religions or all participants.
- Check: Explain what changes in daily activity when a ritual obligation applies.
- Supports [K11-01](BuiltWorld.md#k11-01): Built programme follows the activities a place supports.
- Supports [K08-14](Societies.md#k08-14): A practiced ritual can mark a transition into a newly recognized social role.

## K08-07

**Authority needs a recognized scope.** The ability to compel action differs from an accepted right to command; authority can be limited to an office or domain.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: hierarchy; Authority and legitimacy; Law, custom, courts and punishment.
- Apply: Separate coercive capacity, accepted legitimacy and the matters an institution controls.
- Limit: A uniform, title or monumental building does not establish unanimous acceptance.
- Check: Find a demand the same official can legitimately make and one that would be disputed.
- Supports [K08-02](Societies.md#k08-02): Institutions distribute authority through practices and meaning.
- Supports [K08-13](Societies.md#k08-13): Collection and allocation are powers whose institutional scope can be examined.

## K08-08

**Succession exposes different foundations of legitimacy.** Custom, personal allegiance and formal office can support authority in different ways.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Governance and administration; Institutional decline and succession; contested histories.
- Apply: Ask what happens to a faction's obligations when its leader is replaced.
- Limit: These are analytical distinctions; actual institutions can mix them.
- Check: Trace who obeys the successor, who disputes the claim and why.
- Supports [K09-03](Narrative.md#k09-03): Institutional change creates state-dependent narrative consequences.

## K08-09

**Delayed production needs an intervening means of support.** Some subsistence activities return food quickly; others require labor well before harvest. Societies can combine several such strategies.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: food; Foraging, pastoralism, farming and fishing; Storage and preservation; storage.
- Apply: Account for food, seed, storage and work between planting and harvest when describing settlement life.
- Limit: A dominant subsistence label does not describe every household or imply a fixed social hierarchy.
- Check: Trace how a household eats during the production interval and what a failed harvest changes.
- Requires [K04-08](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-08): Stored provisions and incoming harvests are different quantities.
- Supports [K08-03](Societies.md#k08-03): Production intervals give material objects and routines a functional context.

## K08-10

**Food acquisition and food meaning are different questions.** Foodways include preparation, shared consumption, preferences and prohibitions; available calories alone do not describe eating practices.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: food; foodways; Food, clothing, hygiene and medicine; Variation within cultures.
- Apply: Distinguish how food is obtained from who prepares it, shares it and regards it as appropriate.
- Limit: Members of a society need not observe the same practices or agree on their meaning.
- Check: Describe one meal from procurement to leftovers, including an exception to the dominant practice.
- Requires [K08-05](Societies.md#k08-05): Local meanings must be examined rather than inferred from an outsider's label.
- Supports [K08-06](Societies.md#k08-06): Food practices can intersect with ritual without being reducible to it.

## K08-11

**Descent, residence and authority are separate relations.** Kinship affiliation, co-residence and decision authority need not follow the same line; tracing descent through mothers does not by itself establish rule by women.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: households; Kinship, family and descent; Housing and household organization; Property, inheritance and commons.
- Apply: Specify household membership, residence, inheritance and decision rights separately.
- Limit: A kinship category does not uniquely determine domestic arrangements or power.
- Check: Draw the household and descent relations, then identify who can decide, inherit and relocate.
- Requires [K08-07](Societies.md#k08-07): Authority needs a defined scope rather than being inferred from family terminology.
- Supports [K11-01](BuiltWorld.md#k11-01): Household organization can change which domestic activities share space.

## K08-12

**Gifts can create timed reciprocal obligations.** Balanced reciprocity expects a return of comparable value within an accepted interval; generalized reciprocity need not keep an exact account of each gift.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Trade, barter, money and credit; alliances and dependency; Faction interests and interdependence; values and norms.
- Apply: Define who may give, refuse or return an object, what counts as adequate return, and when a delay strains the relationship.
- Limit: These are analytical distinctions, not universal stages of society or a rule that every gift demands payment.
- Check: Trace the same gift among close relations, trading partners and outsiders; explain any different obligations.
- Requires [K08-05](Societies.md#k08-05): Local meanings establish what an acceptable return represents.
- Supports [K09-11](Narrative.md#k09-11): A known obligation and due interval can establish an anticipated consequence.

## K08-13

**Redistribution needs collection and allocation roles.** Redistribution pools goods or labor through a person or institution before later allocation; it can coexist with reciprocal and market exchanges.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: institutions; Governance and administration; Tax, tribute and redistribution; Faction interests and interdependence.
- Apply: Connect tribute, shared stores and public work to collectors, custodians, recipients and allocation criteria.
- Limit: Central collection does not by itself establish fair distribution, legitimacy or equal access.
- Check: Follow a contribution into storage and onward to a recipient; identify who decides and who may contest the allocation.
- Requires [K08-07](Societies.md#k08-07): An institution's collection and allocation powers need a defined scope.
- Requires [K04-08](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-08): Stored contributions and later releases must be distinguishable flows.

## K08-14

**A rite of passage changes a socially recognized status.** A rite-of-passage model distinguishes leaving an earlier status, an intervening period outside ordinary roles and incorporation into a newly recognized status.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: ritual; Gender and life stages; Birth, adulthood, aging and death; Status, class, caste and mobility.
- Apply: For an initiation or life-stage ceremony, explain the participant's prior role, transitional treatment and changed rights or obligations afterward.
- Limit: The three-part model is an interpretive tool, not a mandatory sequence for every society or proof that bodily change alone changes social status.
- Check: Compare how the community treats the same person before and after the rite; identify who recognizes or disputes the new status.
- Requires [K08-07](Societies.md#k08-07): A changed social status needs an account of who recognizes its rights and obligations.
- Constrains [K08-15](Societies.md#k08-15): A communal ritual can reaffirm membership without moving participants into a new status.

## K08-15

**A communal ritual can reaffirm membership without promotion.** Rites of intensification can reinforce belonging and shared identity through collective preparation and participation rather than a change of individual status.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: ritual; Ritual, devotion and moral practice; Work, leisure, games and festivals.
- Apply: For a recurring festival, distinguish the work of preparing together from the public ceremony and from any individual initiation within it.
- Limit: Shared participation does not establish uniform belief, universal inclusion or a guaranteed cooperative outcome.
- Check: Identify who prepares, participates or is excluded, then compare the claimed sense of belonging with actual obligations and disagreements.
- Requires [K08-06](Societies.md#k08-06): Belonging must be related to a practiced activity rather than an emblem alone.
- Constrains [K05-01](CooperativePlay.md#k05-01): Feeling united does not by itself create the task dependencies of cooperative play.
