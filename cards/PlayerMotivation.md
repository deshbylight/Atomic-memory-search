## K02-16

**A goal compares a present state with a desired end state.** Goal-directed motivation can be understood as comparing a current state with a desired end state and directing behavior toward reducing that difference.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Goals, needs, expectations and perceived competence.
- Apply: Describe what the player wants to change, what action can change it and how progress or completion becomes perceptible.
- Limit: A comparison model does not explain every motive or guarantee that a stated objective matters to a player.
- Check: Ask a participant to explain the desired end state and whether the last action brought it nearer; revise an unreadable or irrelevant objective.
- Supports [K04-14](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-14): The value of another reward depends on which need or goal it can still advance.
- Requires [K02-01](PlayerExperience.md#k02-01): A useful progress comparison needs a cue the player can distinguish and retrieve.

## K02-17

**Personal and social motives can direct the same activity.** Behavior can serve achievement, social acceptance or risk approach and avoidance, alongside biological needs; one activity need not have a single motive.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: belonging; Goals, needs, expectations and perceived competence.
- Apply: For an optional shared challenge, distinguish seeking mastery, staying with companions and avoiding loss before predicting who will participate.
- Limit: These are possible explanations, not fixed player types; a fictional hunger meter does not establish a real participant need.
- Check: Compare participants who take and decline the same challenge; ask what each was trying to achieve rather than inferring motive from participation alone.
- Constrains [K02-02](PlayerExperience.md#k02-02): Equivalent stated stakes can matter differently when participants pursue different ends.
- Requires [K16-01](ResearchAndProduction.md#k16-01): A proposed motive needs observations that distinguish it from competing explanations.

## K02-18

**Enjoying an activity differs from valuing its separate outcome.** Intrinsic motivation concerns benefits within doing an activity; extrinsic motivation concerns benefits separable from the activity itself.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Intrinsic and extrinsic motivation.
- Apply: Distinguish enjoyment of navigating a route from wanting the item at its end before changing the reward to solve disengagement.
- Limit: Both can coexist. A reward is not inherently harmful, and voluntary participation alone does not establish intrinsic enjoyment.
- Check: Ask what the participant enjoyed doing and what outcome they wanted; compare those answers with the observed choices.
- Supports [K02-17](PlayerMotivation.md#k02-17): The same activity can serve enjoyment, achievement and social motives in different combinations.
- Constrains [K04-01](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-01): A larger external reward does not establish that the activity itself became more enjoyable.

## K02-19

**A valued goal can still feel unattainable.** Commitment to a goal can depend on both its perceived value and the expectation that it can be achieved.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Goals, needs, expectations and perceived competence.
- Apply: When a participant declines an objective, distinguish wanting its result from believing the available actions can reach it.
- Limit: This is a motivational account, not a universal numerical balance formula; perceived attainability can differ from actual difficulty.
- Check: Ask separately whether the outcome matters and whether the participant sees a feasible path to it; reject an explanation that combines those answers into one score.
- Requires [K02-16](PlayerMotivation.md#k02-16): Assessing attainability requires a desired end state and actions that could reduce the gap.
- Requires [K16-05](ResearchAndProduction.md#k16-05): A single willingness score can hide distinct problems of goal value and perceived attainability.

## K02-20

**Securing an obligation differs from pursuing an aspiration.** A prevention focus emphasizes security and avoiding losses; a promotion focus emphasizes advancement and attaining gains. Similar actions can serve either orientation.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Goals, needs, expectations and perceived competence.
- Apply: Distinguish preserving an existing supply from expanding future possibilities when explaining why the same expedition matters to different participants.
- Limit: Orientations vary with person and situation; they are not permanent player classes or a guarantee that loss framing motivates everyone.
- Check: Ask what a participant is trying to preserve or advance, then check whether the proposed feedback communicates that consequence.
- Supports [K02-17](PlayerMotivation.md#k02-17): Observed participation does not uniquely identify the end a participant is pursuing.
- Constrains [K02-02](PlayerExperience.md#k02-02): The effect of a gain or loss description depends partly on the goal orientation of its audience.

## K02-21

**Pleasantness and arousal describe different aspects of feeling.** Affect can be described along pleasantness and arousal dimensions; excitement and calm can both be pleasant while differing in arousal.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Appraisal, arousal and emotional regulation.
- Apply: Describe a rest scene as intended calm or an escape as intended excitement rather than treating both as one positive-emotion target.
- Limit: These dimensions do not identify every discrete emotion, and low arousal alone does not demonstrate boredom or disengagement.
- Check: Ask separately how pleasant and how activated the experience felt; reject a success metric that treats all low-arousal reports as failure.
- Constrains [K16-05](ResearchAndProduction.md#k16-05): A single intensity measure can confuse the intended emotional quality with arousal.
- Supports [K02-22](PlayerMotivation.md#k02-22): Comparing desired and experienced feeling requires specifying which emotional dimensions are being compared.

## K02-22

**Desired emotional states differ from experienced states.** How someone wants to feel can differ from how they currently feel; cultural experience can influence the emotional states they value.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Social and cultural variation in emotional interpretation.
- Apply: Record the feeling participants seek as well as the feeling they report after a concept probe, especially when choosing calm or excitement.
- Limit: Population averages do not determine an individual preference. Cultural context is not a substitute for asking the intended audience.
- Check: Compare desired and experienced states for the same participant before interpreting a quiet or excited response as concept success.
- Requires [K02-04](PlayerExperience.md#k02-04): Audience context and individual differences must constrain emotional generalizations.
- Requires [K02-21](PlayerMotivation.md#k02-21): Pleasantness and arousal help distinguish wanted calm from wanted excitement.
