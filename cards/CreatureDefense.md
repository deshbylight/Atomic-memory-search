## K13-28

**A rigid exoskeleton needs a growth process.** An arthropod exoskeleton provides protection and muscle attachment, but growth requires forming a new covering and shedding the old one.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: defence; Fur, feathers, scales and skin; Life stage, sex and population variation.
- Apply: Include a growth stage and a plausible shed covering in the creature lifecycle.
- Limit: Molting does not by itself establish a combat vulnerability or its duration.
- Check: Increase body size and explain how the rigid covering accommodates the change.
- Constrains [K13-01](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-01): A rigid covering must still permit the required body movements.
- Requires [K07-11](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-11): Growing inhabitants need resources as well as a new covering.

## K13-29

**Small endotherms face different heat and food costs.** A smaller endotherm generally has more surface relative to its mass and greater heat loss per mass, increasing the food demand of maintaining body temperature.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: functional proportions; Habitat, climate and energy demands.
- Apply: Compare the same warm-bodied creature at two sizes in a cold habitat.
- Limit: Insulation, activity and surroundings matter; the pattern is not a universal ectotherm rule.
- Check: Account for insulation and food before assigning identical cold survival at both sizes.
- Constrains [K07-11](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-11): Size-dependent food demand changes how many inhabitants resources can sustain.
- Constrains [K07-16](EcologicalCommunities.md#k07-16): Thermoregulation adds demand to the available food-energy input.

## K13-30

**Concealment and warning coloration serve different defenses.** Camouflage can reduce detection against a background, while warning coloration can advertise an actual defense to predators that perceive and respond to it.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: defence; Fur, feathers, scales and skin.
- Apply: Name the predator and background before choosing muted or conspicuous markings.
- Limit: Bright color alone proves neither toxicity nor a learned avoidance response.
- Check: Change the background or predator sensory system and reconsider the protection.
- Requires [K13-20](CreatureSenses.md#k13-20): The receiver must detect the proposed visual distinction.
- Requires [K13-22](CreatureSenses.md#k13-22): The warning pattern must remain distinguishable at the intended light level.

## K13-31

**Shared warning patterns need a predator relationship.** A relatively undefended mimic can resemble a defended species, or several defended species can share a warning pattern; the protection depends on predator response.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: defence; Fur, feathers, scales and skin.
- Apply: Distinguish the model, mimic and shared predator before reusing a visual pattern.
- Limit: Resemblance alone establishes neither mimicry nor common ancestry.
- Check: Remove the relevant predator response and explain what protective benefit remains.
- Requires [K13-30](CreatureDefense.md#k13-30): Mimicry needs the warning signal and defense roles distinguished.
- Requires [K07-02](PhysicalWorld.md#k07-02): The proposed protection is a relationship among organisms.
