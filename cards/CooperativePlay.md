## K05-01

**Interdependence needs a shared outcome.** A group involves mutual influence and shared purpose; membership alone does not explain who needs whom or how contributions combine.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: goal alignment; dependency.
- Apply: For a cooperative objective identify what each participant changes and how another participant benefits from or responds to it.
- Limit: This does not approve rigid classes, party size or a mandatory specialist gate.
- Check: Remove one contribution in a paper walkthrough: does a cooperative decision change, or only total damage?
- Supports [K03-03](ActionsAndCombat.md#k03-03): Distinct contributions can alter approach, visibility or retreat rather than only damage.
- Requires [K05-09](CooperativePlay.md#k05-09): A shared outcome can still distribute benefits in conflicting ways.
- Constrains [K05-12](CooperativePlay.md#k05-12): Shared purpose does not determine whether discussion moderates or strengthens a group preference.

## K05-02

**Communication structure creates bottlenecks.** A central communication point can simplify coordination but also become a bottleneck; decentralized access changes who can act on information.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: shared information; coordination load.
- Apply: Assign who sees a cue, who can share it and who is allowed to act; provide a usable non-voice path for essential coordination.
- Limit: More messages do not guarantee shared understanding; group size and task structure change coordination cost.
- Check: Run an essential decision with voice disabled and one participant seeing the cue late.
- Requires [K15-04](InteractionAndAccess.md#k15-04): Shared state and usable controls determine whether information reaches the group.

## K05-03

**Exploration information can diverge.** Group members can possess different information and exercise mutual influence; a decision available to one member may not be available to another.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: splitting and reunion.
- Apply: Trace who discovers, recognizes and communicates a route; show how separated players can describe a meeting point.
- Limit: Information asymmetry is useful only when recovering or sharing it remains meaningful; do not assume every split is enjoyable.
- Check: Ask two separated players to agree on a destination without a developer explaining the map.
- Requires [K02-01](PlayerExperience.md#k02-01): Shared directions rely on distinctive retrievable location cues.

## K05-04

**Group cost and control have owners.** Shared work can suffer from weak accountability, unequal participation and conflicts between individual and group goals.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: shared costs; consent.
- Apply: For a group-affecting action identify who pays, who consents, who can reverse it and who loses access after the decision.
- Limit: Use the game's own shared-versus-unique loot rules; do not invent coefficients or silently grant one player authority over others.
- Check: Walk through an impatient player taking the reward or triggering the exit before a slower participant arrives.
- Constrains [K15-04](InteractionAndAccess.md#k15-04): Cooperative interfaces must communicate ownership and irreversible group effects.

## K05-05

**Shared benefit can reward noncontribution.** When contributors pay a private cost but everyone receives the benefit, declining to contribute can be individually attractive.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Shared and private resources, risks and rewards; Payoff structures and competing incentives; Cooperation dilemmas and free riding; shared costs.
- Apply: Write each participant's cost and benefit before calling a shared objective cooperative.
- Limit: Payoff structure alone does not predict actual behavior; norms and concern for others also matter.
- Check: Compare contributing and withholding under the same partner actions.
- Constrains [K05-01](CooperativePlay.md#k05-01): A shared outcome does not by itself align individual incentives.
- Requires [K04-05](EconomyAndProgression.md#k04-05): A contribution cost includes the contributor's forgone alternative use.

## K05-06

**Coordination loss differs from effort loss.** A group can lose performance through poor synchronization or reduced individual effort; these require different explanations.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: unequal contribution; Team size, contribution visibility and social loafing; Coordination demands versus individual task demands; Evaluation of coordination cost and shared experience.
- Apply: Observe missed handoffs separately from idle participation.
- Limit: Public contribution scores can omit support work and introduce unwanted competition.
- Check: Identify whether a failed task lacked information, timing, skill or effort before changing rewards.
- Requires [K05-02](CooperativePlay.md#k05-02): Communication bottlenecks can resemble motivation problems.
- Requires [K05-13](CooperativePlay.md#k05-13): When judging contribution, include the communication and maintenance functions the group needs.

## K05-07

**Agreement can conceal missing information.** Pressure for consensus can suppress disagreement and reduce the search for alternatives.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: consensus; Confidence, speaking space and domination; Disruptive behavior, exclusion and conflict prevention.
- Apply: Provide a way to state uncertainty and share private observations before commitment.
- Limit: Unanimity does not prove that everyone has the same information or preference.
- Check: Ask quieter participants what evidence or option was never discussed.
- Requires [K05-03](CooperativePlay.md#k05-03): Different discoveries must be communicated before shared decisions.
- Requires [K05-11](CooperativePlay.md#k05-11): When evaluating apparent consensus, distinguish public agreement from private endorsement.
- Constrains [K05-15](CooperativePlay.md#k05-15): A quiet or unanimous group can still have suppressed disagreements that conflict diagnosis must consider.

## K05-08

**Newcomer learning competes with group pace.** A difficult unfamiliar task can become harder under social evaluation even when experienced teammates perform it fluently.

- Kind: design inference. Focus: different curiosity and expertise; Friend groups, strangers, newcomers and skill gaps; Onboarding into an existing group.
- Apply: Observe a newcomer joining an established group and distinguish learning time from mandatory waiting.
- Limit: This is a design inference; it does not justify automatic catch-up rewards or changing solo rules.
- Check: Check whether the newcomer understands a decision before the group commits.
- Requires [K15-04](InteractionAndAccess.md#k15-04): Late readers need a legible account of shared state.

## K05-09

**Efficiency does not decide fairness.** An allocation is Pareto efficient when no feasible change benefits someone without harming another; efficient allocations can still be unequal.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: conflicting preferences; Fair allocation and perceived fairness.
- Apply: Record who gains and loses instead of judging a shared reward only by its total.
- Limit: The criterion depends on participants' preferences and cannot rank every conflicting allocation.
- Check: Find two efficient allocations that distribute benefits differently; state the separate fairness criterion.
- Requires [K05-04](CooperativePlay.md#k05-04): Costs, benefits and control need identifiable owners.
- Requires [K02-02](PlayerExperience.md#k02-02): A claim about perceived fairness requires examining how alternatives are presented.

## K05-10

**Future interaction changes cooperation incentives.** When future interactions matter, an immediate gain from defection can be outweighed by losing later cooperation.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Repeated interaction and learning about partners; trust and reciprocity; Trust, reputation, reciprocity and prosocial behavior.
- Apply: Compare a one-time anonymous encounter with continuing interaction where prior conduct is observable.
- Limit: Repetition does not guarantee trust; future value, observability and responses to mistakes matter.
- Check: Compare the incentives when future contact is likely, unlikely and impossible.
- Constrains [K05-05](CooperativePlay.md#k05-05): A one-round contribution dilemma does not describe all repeated incentives.
- Requires [K05-03](CooperativePlay.md#k05-03): Participants can respond only to conduct they can observe or learn about.

## K05-11

**Public agreement can differ from private belief.** People may align with a group to gain acceptance or because they believe it has better information; outward conformity does not establish private endorsement.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: consensus; private information; Public and private information; Group formation, norms, roles and cohesion; Confidence, speaking space and domination.
- Apply: Distinguish a shared judgment from agreement expressed after hearing others, especially when one participant speaks first or appears authoritative.
- Limit: These are possible influences, not evidence that every agreement is coerced or mistaken.
- Check: Compare independent private judgments with the later public decision and ask which information, if any, changed the judgment.
- Constrains [K05-07](CooperativePlay.md#k05-07): Consensus can reflect social pressure without resolving the search for alternatives.
- Requires [K05-03](CooperativePlay.md#k05-03): Private and shared information must be distinguished before interpreting agreement.
- Requires [K05-14](CooperativePlay.md#k05-14): When authority influences agreement, distinguish relevant expertise from an accepted right to direct.

## K05-12

**Discussion can strengthen an initial group preference.** Group polarization is a shift toward a stronger version of an initial group attitude after discussion, rather than automatic movement toward a moderate compromise.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: conflicting preferences; consensus; Group formation, norms, roles and cohesion.
- Apply: Compare preferences before and after discussion when interpreting a group's chosen risk, route or concept.
- Limit: Polarization is a possible result, not a universal direction of change; unanimous preference does not establish that the option is better.
- Check: Record initial individual positions and the later group position; distinguish stronger commitment from newly supplied evidence.
- Requires [K05-11](CooperativePlay.md#k05-11): Private judgments and public declarations can differ before and after discussion.
- Constrains [K02-04](PlayerExperience.md#k02-04): A group preference cannot automatically stand in for the preferences of isolated participants.

## K05-13

**Communication roles are functions that members can share.** Task roles help a group progress, while maintenance roles support its relationships and participation. One member can perform several functions, and multiple members can share a function.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: role flexibility; Group formation, norms, roles and cohesion.
- Apply: Observe who clarifies information, tracks decisions, invites quiet members and repairs tension, independently of combat or equipment roles.
- Limit: These categories describe behaviors, not fixed personalities or proof that every group needs one permanent specialist per function.
- Check: Let the usual organizer step back and observe which functions transfer, disappear or become duplicated.
- Constrains [K05-06](CooperativePlay.md#k05-06): Apparently low output can conceal communication and relationship work omitted by a narrow contribution measure.
- Supports [K05-07](CooperativePlay.md#k05-07): Inviting missing information is a group function that can counter premature consensus.

## K05-14

**An appointed leader need not hold the relevant expertise.** Expert influence rests on knowledge relevant to a task; legitimate authority rests on an accepted right to direct action within a domain.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: leadership; Leadership, power and influence.
- Apply: Formal rank does not supply task knowledge: compare an appointed leader's decision rights with the knowledge held by the member who understands the current obstacle.
- Limit: Neither a title nor confidence establishes relevant expertise; expertise in one task does not establish a general right to command.
- Check: Change the problem while keeping the same participants and titles; ask why the same person should still direct the decision.
- Requires [K08-07](Societies.md#k08-07): Accepted authority has a scope that differs from mere capacity to compel.
- Constrains [K05-11](CooperativePlay.md#k05-11): Following an instruction does not establish private agreement with its judgment.

## K05-15

**Conflict diagnosis needs the actual object of disagreement.** Group disagreement can concern facts, values, procedures, misread intentions or relationships. These sources can overlap and change during an exchange.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Conflict, negotiation and repair; conflicting preferences.
- Apply: For a disputed route or reward decision, identify whether participants dispute evidence, priorities, process or how they were treated before choosing a response.
- Limit: Clarifying a fact cannot by itself reconcile opposed values; a classification is a working hypothesis, not a diagnosis of a participant.
- Check: Ask each side what would need to change for agreement, then test whether the proposed response addresses that stated difference.
- Requires [K05-03](CooperativePlay.md#k05-03): Determine what each participant actually knows before attributing disagreement to incompatible preferences.
- Constrains [K05-11](CooperativePlay.md#k05-11): Silencing an expressed dispute does not demonstrate a change in private judgment.
