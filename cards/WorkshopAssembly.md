## K12-22

**A screw joint needs engagement and removal access.** A wood screw can draw parts together when its head bears on the outer part and its thread engages the receiving part; accessible screws can permit later dismantling.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: construction; Assembly, joints, fasteners and load paths; Storage, maintenance, repair and reuse.
- Apply: Separate the clearance passage, threaded engagement and screwdriver approach in a prop assembly.
- Limit: Damaged heads, concealed fasteners, adhesive or poor thread purchase can prevent the proposed removal.
- Check: Trace both tightening and removal without passing the driver through another part.
- Requires [K12-08](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-08): The retained wood parts must still tolerate their dimensional movement.
- Supports [K12-13](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-13): Fastener access gives a different reason for dismantling than a removable joint key.

## K12-23

**Trial assembly exposes fit before bonding.** A dry assembly can reveal fit, alignment and the required joining sequence before adhesive sets; preparation matters when the bonding operation has limited working time.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: construction; Assembly, joints, fasteners and load paths; Storage, maintenance, repair and reuse.
- Apply: Lay out the parts and supports of a repaired prop before describing the finished seam.
- Limit: Adhesives differ in gap tolerance and cure behavior; one historical glue process does not define every bond.
- Check: Reconstruct the assembly order and identify the part that becomes inaccessible first.
- Requires [K12-08](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-08): A panel intended to move must not be accidentally fixed by the bonding plan.
- Supports [K12-03](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-03): A shared assembly sequence can explain recurring seams and repair differences in a prop family.

## K12-24

**A hinge position changes the opening clearance.** The position of a hinge pin relative to adjoining surfaces affects how far a lid or door can open before its parts meet.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Object purpose and user action; Tools, containers, furniture and machinery; Assembly, joints, fasteners and load paths.
- Apply: Draw the hinge axis and sweep the connected parts through the intended opening.
- Limit: Hinge styles have different stops and offsets; a decorative hinge shape does not establish its motion.
- Check: Check intermediate angles and the fully open position for unwanted contact.
- Requires [K13-09](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-09): Intermediate positions reveal contact that closed and open endpoints can miss.
- Supports [K13-14](CharactersAndMotion.md#k13-14): An opening in fitted equipment needs a feasible clearance around the wearer.

## K12-25

**Abrasive strokes leave directional surface evidence.** Sanding can leave scratches aligned with the working stroke; cross-grain marks differ from grain-following marks, while the support behind the abrasive affects surface evenness.

- Kind: general guidance. Focus: Wood species, grain, cutting and finishing; Mechanical abrasion and contact wear.
- Apply: Give a finished or reworked wooden surface marks consistent with the grain and reachable stroke.
- Limit: Finishing marks are not automatically wear from ordinary use, and later coatings can obscure them.
- Check: Compare the proposed stroke, grain direction and exposed scratch pattern before assigning random damage.
- Requires [K12-14](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-14): The grain direction must be established before comparing it with abrasive marks.
- Supports [K12-12](EnvironmentArt.md#k12-12): Overlapping finishing and damage marks can establish a relative event order.
