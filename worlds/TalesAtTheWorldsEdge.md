# World record: the tales at the edge of the world

Kind: world-record
Summary: A collection of short tales about thieves, idols and dreamers near the edge of the world, in which wealth is bait, houses turn their backs on what they fear, and rivals bring their own roof down.

Record of a fictional world read in whole, from the preface to the last page of the epilogue; only the publisher's header, the contents list and the licence text were left out. The book is a collection of separate tales that share a place and a manner, some of them starting in a modern city, and not one continuous history. Confidence: `high` = stated in the text; `medium` = inferred from what the text shows.

## World in one paragraph

Beyond the fields we know the world goes on to an edge, and most of these tales are errands to that edge. A jeweller goes after a gem in a spider idol's shrine; a band of thieves creeps to a guarded box beside a crack in the world held shut with an iron clamp; a man with a grievance climbs down a stair past the last of the gods. Man-eaters in a tower keep a vast hoard for no reason except that it fills their larder. Burglars die in a wood whose nearest village has no window facing it. A rider on a winged beast sees a twilight city whose every window looks at the world and none at the far greater city behind it. Two idols in one temple cancel each other until, for once, they pull the same way and the roof falls on both. A clerk's window opens on a walled city that he watches fall.
