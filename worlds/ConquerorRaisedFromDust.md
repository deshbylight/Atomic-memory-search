# World record: the conqueror raised from the dust

Kind: world-record
Summary: Plotters wake a sorcerer dead for three thousand years with a burning stone, win a kingdom with his help and lose it again, while a deposed king crosses half the world after the stolen stone that is the only thing able to send the sorcerer back.

Record of a fictional world read in whole, from the first line of the story to its last; only the publisher's header and the licence text were left out. Real faiths, peoples and places named in the text are left out, and cultures are described only by what they do. Acts of cruelty are named only as far as a rule needs them. Confidence: `high` = stated in the text; `medium` = inferred from what the text shows.

## World in one paragraph

Four plotters lay a red stone on a mummy and a conqueror of a fallen empire breathes again. With his arts they break a western king's army: a plague is called and dismissed, the king is held in his tent, a stand-in dies in his armour, and cliffs fall on the loyal host. The king escapes the pits under the conquerors' palace and rides home in secret. A frightened priest has meanwhile stolen the stone from the sleeping sorcerer and sent it away to be lost in the sea. A forest witch shows the king that only the stone can stop the sorcerer, and that it lies under a shrine he must reclaim. He follows it from hand to hand down to a southern port, loses it to a rival priest, and takes it back from a hall of the dead under an older pyramid. At home the people rise, a priest of a hunted sect uses the stone on a hilltop altar, and the sorcerer falls back into dust.

## Unresolved

- Whether the stone ever worked for anyone who did not shed blood for it.
- Why the shrine over the cave was rebuilt each time on the same place.
- What the old-blooded hill people would have become had the sorcerer won.
- How the hunted sect learned of the king's return.
