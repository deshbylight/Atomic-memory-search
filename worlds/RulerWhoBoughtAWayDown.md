# World record: the ruler who bought a way down

Kind: world-record
Summary: A ruler who wants to know everything pays a hideous stranger in lives for a door under the earth, keeps the terms by their letter, breaks them anyway, and is let in, to find that every door opens and that nothing behind them can be wanted any more.

Record of a fictional world read in whole, from the first line of the story to its last; only the publisher's header, the editor's biographical introduction and the licence text were left out. Real faiths, peoples and places named in the text are left out, and cultures are described only by what they do. Acts of cruelty are named only as far as a rule needs them. Confidence: `high` = stated in the text; `medium` = inferred from what the text shows.

## World in one paragraph

A ruler with a look that can fell a person builds a palace for each sense and a tower of eleven thousand stairs to read the stars. A hideous stranger sells him blades with writing that changes from day to day, and offers a door under the earth with all the treasure of the kings before people. The price is paid in lives at the edge of a chasm, and the chasm shuts. Fresh terms arrive: travel to a far ruin and enter no dwelling on the way. The ruler's train is broken in the mountains by beasts and fire. He stops at a pious refuge, takes the daughter of the house, who has been hidden from him by a staged funeral, and goes on after his mother rides out to drive him. A last warning empties his following. Below the ruin the terms are waived and he is let in. In a hall without end every door opens to him, and a crowd crosses it, each one alone with a burning heart, the state he too will reach within days.

## Unresolved

- What the changing writing said on the days nobody read it.
- Who did the night work on the tower, beyond the narrator's word.
- Why the eaters of the dead know what the mother asks of them.
