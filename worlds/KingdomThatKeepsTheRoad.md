# World record: the kingdom that keeps an old road

Kind: world-record
Summary: A hidden kingdom behind a waterless plain and a mountain wall keeps up a road it did not build, seats its dead kings under a dripping roof, and guards a slab door that almost nobody can work.

Record of a fictional world read in whole, from the narrator's introduction to the last page of the story; only the publisher's header and licence text were left out. The narrator's remarks about real peoples are left out, and the cultures here are described only by what they do. Confidence: `high` = stated in the text; `medium` = inferred from what the text shows.

## World in one paragraph

Three hunters cross a waterless plain and a mountain wall to find a lost man, following a chart that a dying explorer wrote in his own blood centuries before. Behind the wall a kingdom keeps a great road nobody living could build. Its king holds power by a yearly purge and by regiments drilled in blocks, until a claimant from among the visitors' own servants raises half of them and the king falls after a battle round a horseshoe hill. At the road's end are a vast pit, three seated giants, and a cave where dead kings sit turning to stone under the drip. Behind them a stone slab hides a store left in haste long ago. An impossibly old woman who works it shuts the party in and is crushed by it; the party gets out through forgotten workings, comes up near the bottom of the pit, and the way in is never found again.

## Unresolved

- How each king opened the door if she alone held its secret.
- Who built the road, the pit and the giants, and why they left in haste.
- Whether the old woman is the same one named in the explorer's account.
- Whether the seated figure began the custom or merely frightened thieves.
